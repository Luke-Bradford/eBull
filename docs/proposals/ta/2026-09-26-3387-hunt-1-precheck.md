## Result
For every hunt-1 construction, the published point estimate of its edge is **below** the harness's own promotion
bar (Bar B below), or above it by a margin that the known haircuts (post-publication decay, a different population,
the price-only cell) each remove. A trial whose expected active mean under stress is ≤ 0 has P(t > 3) no higher
than the null's (~0.1%) at any variance, so it cannot fund the pot; its only lasting effect is +1 on M, which
raises the bar for every later trial, programme-wide and permanently.

**Decision (a budget choice, not a proof of impossibility): hunt 1 registers no trial and closes** (harness spec:
"a hunt with no searches closes with no register entry"). The closing statement is "no demonstrated edge", an
administrative closure: these families were screened out, not tested and rejected. A real edge larger than its
published estimate remains possible and unexamined. What the screen does establish is that **harness v1's full
round trips plus its stress rule set a 3.5–14%/yr active-edge bar on short-horizon families**, which is what
hunt 2 is built on.

## The two bars every candidate must clear
Harness v1 exits every cohort at x (horizon cap 63 sessions), so a cohort is a full round trip, (252 / h) a year.
Frozen round-trip spread bands (`cost_model.BANDS`, p75, %): ≥ $100 0.322, $20–100 0.509, $5–20 0.571,
< $5 1.450. UK tariff: $0 commission on US stocks (`hunt_harness.HUNT_TARIFF`). Figures below are
arithmetic, non-compounded %/yr, on today's bands applied to history (the harness's labelled counterfactual).

**Bar A, tracker comparator (queue rule 2(b)).** Net of costs the strategy must beat a cap-weighted tracker, which
trades almost never. Arm − tracker = (arm − control) + (control − tracker); both books pay the band × 252/h, so
unless the control itself beats the tracker gross, the arm's active edge must cover **band × 252/h + 1.5%/yr**
(the declared worthwhile edge). ⚠ No tracker series is in the harness yet; this bar is arithmetic, not a test. In general the
hurdle is E > 1.5 + k_arm − k_tracker − g, with g the gross control-minus-tracker return; the table takes
k_tracker ≈ 0 and g = 0, and no evidence here says g ≤ 0.

All figures are approximate: fill-price accounting makes a round trip cost s/(1 + s/2) at an unchanged price, idle
slots and missing entries lower realised turnover, a mixed-band book pays a weighted charge, and the harness's
uncosted rebalancing between slots is not in them.

**Bar B, the harness's own `PASS` (spec "Verdicts", "Lanes and cost model").** A `PASS` needs every **stress**
cell at t > 3 and mean(a) > 0, and stress charges **arm** positions the top band (1.450) while the control keeps its
base band. So mean(a) > 0 under stress needs an active edge above **(1.450 − base band) × 252/h**. Failing only
this bar, with every base cell passing, gives `PASS_CONTINGENT`: "no capital, no holdout".

| h | ≥ $100: A / B | $20–100: A / B | $5–20: A / B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 5.6 / 14.2 | 7.9 / 11.9 | 8.7 / 11.1 |
| 21 | 5.4 / 13.5 | 7.6 / 11.3 | 8.4 / 10.5 |
| 63 | 2.8 / 4.5 | 3.5 / 3.8 | 3.8 / 3.5 |

(A includes the 1.5%/yr margin; B is the mean-only condition, before t > 3.) Names below $5 are excluded from every
candidate: at 1.450 round trip their Bar A alone is 18.9%/yr at h = 21.

A four-factor alpha is neither excess over the tracker nor arm − matched control, and GKM's reference portfolio is
not the harness control; each published figure is used as the best available point estimate of the arm's active
mean, and a filtered subset (≥ $5, a price band) can differ from it in either direction.

**Why 3b is out, although 13.8%/yr exceeds Bar B's 10.5–13.5:**
- the margin is 0.3–3.3%/yr **before** any haircut, so power ≥ 0.5 at the stress gate needs a stress-cell IR
  ≥ 0.86, i.e. a tracking error of at most 0.35 / 2.9 / 3.8%/yr by band, which a basket of tens of names rotating
  monthly is unlikely to have (unmeasured: it cannot be measured without a look);
- as a scenario, McLean & Pontiff's (2016) **average** post-publication decline of 58% (`strategy-evidence.md`
  §2.6; an average over predictors, not a measured haircut for this one) leaves 5.8%/yr, below Bar B at every band;
- H&S's sample is US common shares and **excludes ADRs**; our semi-annual population is not restricted that way
  (the archive carries no share-type field in the view), so the published figure is for a different population;
- H&S's estimation period runs to 2011, overlapping our 2009–2021 validation window, so validation would re-test
  data the choice was informed by;
- the harness's price-only base cell (`hunt_inference.screening_p` takes the worst base cell, and one base cell
  omits dividends) handicaps this arm by the **differential** dividend it holds through (arm capture vs control
  capture, both unmeasured). It is a handicap, not an impossibility: price pressure before the ex-date is part of
  the proposed mechanism. Replacing that cell with a tax-haircut cell was considered and not adopted: Codex ckpt-1
  (finding 27) judged it both a plausible modelling correction and partly the removal of a robustness requirement,
  and this loop does not weaken a promotion gate on its own authority;
- the paper's calendar-month portfolio is not an h = 21 rolling cohort, whose holdings straddle pre-event gains
  and post-event reversals differently;
- withholding at source on foreign issuers' dividends and ADR depositary fees are charged by neither cell.

Semi-annual payer counts, for the record (a heuristic: an ex-date 150–215 calendar days after the previous non-zero

## Power (queue rule 1)
Not reached: power is asked of a candidate that could promote on success, and none can. For the record, a future
trial under the current register needs, at BY q = 0.05 with m = 377 inherited (`TRIAL_REGISTER.inherited_floor()`,
`trial-register-2026-09-26-r13`) + its own count, p ≤ ~2.0e-5 at rank one (BY is step-up, so a second
candidate can reject at a higher rank without meeting this) (Student-t, ~113 block df: t ≈ 4.3, an
annualised IR near 1.0 over 19 discovery years, on the HAC long-run variance); and IR ≥ 0.86 over ~12.2 validation
years for 50% power on the t > 3 gate alone. These are snapshots of a programme-wide count.

## What this means for the programme (hunt 2 onward)
- **Harness v1 structurally favours low turnover.** At h ≤ 63 with full round trips, Bar B requires 3.5–4.5%/yr
  active edge at h = 63 and 10.6–14.2%/yr at monthly horizons. Short-horizon price and volume effects are what the
  turnover filter (`strategy-evidence.md` §2.1, Novy-Marx & Velikov) predicts will fail; the published magnitudes
  agree. Each later family spec runs this table **first**, before any construction work.
- **Families that can fit:** family 5 (regime / crash overlay on the index sleeve) and family 4 as an avoid-list
  inside a held book. Neither is expressible in harness v1 today (an overlay changes exposure over time, not the
  selection; an ETF lane and index-CFD hedge are unpriced), and an avoid-list adds turnover whenever it forces a
  sale. Hunt 2's spec must say how each is costed and compared before it registers anything.
- **Stress definition.** Bar B charges every arm name the < $5 band. That is the harness's frozen, reviewed
  sensitivity and it stays; a candidate whose edge is real but smaller than that charge cannot `PASS`, by design.
