# #3385 spec v3 — Codex ckpt-1 findings (verbatim)

The numbered list the v4 spec's "Implementation obligations" cites. `S:<line>` refers to spec v3 (`b5721c43`).

1. **S:221,224** — Close-entry cohorts return both “0” and “the entry cost only.” These disagree whenever entry costs are nonzero.
2. **S:224,227** — For h = 1, entry and exit rules both apply; the exit rule incorrectly references a previous close. Combining the intraday return and both costs is unspecified.
3. **S:224,227; cost_model.py:28** — Subtracting costs contradicts the reused model’s adjusted-fill convention. Entry costs belong in the acquisition-price denominator; exit costs scale sale proceeds.
4. **S:241** — Proportional costs do not guarantee returns ≥ −100% under the stated subtraction rule: −99.9% minus 0.2% equals −100.1%.
5. **S:229** — Omitting an unavailable position reallocates its capital to tradable names despite the unavailable position remaining held. This is not a self-financing equal-weight book.
6. **S:230** — A returning name’s entire gap return receives its reappearance-day weight, rather than the exposure actually carried through the gap.
7. **S:229** — A cohort with entered positions but no bars on a particular session has an empty mean. Only cohorts with no entered positions are explicitly declared idle.
8. **S:231** — No-entry positions transfer their allocation to other names rather than cash. Arm and control can therefore acquire different exposure and financing assumptions after formation.
9. **S:129,224** — Price validity is specified only at formation. Invalid entry, intermediate, or exit bars have no handling rule.
10. **S:233,394** — Selling at the last close before a missing exit bar requires knowing future bar absence. Removing late exits has introduced retrospective execution.
11. **S:79,233,394** — “Exit is always at x” contradicts charging the exit on an earlier last-bar session. The revision’s timeline fix does not hold.
12. **S:233** — A missing exit can move liquidation back to entry, creating a zero-duration position despite the positive-duration requirement.
13. **S:233** — If the last available close precedes a requested open exit, the replacement changes both the session and execution point; precedence over the declared points is unspecified.
14. **S:225,230** — The displayed formula includes only div(d), while the missing-bar rule requires accumulated dividends across the gap. No accumulation formula is supplied.
15. **S:230,233** — Dividends during a terminal missing-bar gap have no booking rule when no subsequent bar arrives before exit.
16. **S:226,230** — A gap containing splits and multiple dividends needs each cash flow converted onto a common share basis; “the same split ratio” does not define that conversion.
17. **S:226** — Missing dividend records are not distinguished from verified zero dividends; “with dividends” can silently be incomplete.
18. **S:226** — Non-finite, negative, duplicate, or conflicting dividend records have no explicit rejection or reconciliation rule.
19. **S:235** — Any trailing data gap becomes an economic termination, including suspended trading, incomplete ingestion, or an exchange-calendar mismatch.
20. **S:235** — Historical termination classification uses future archive coverage. Freezing that future information makes the result reproducible, not causal.
21. **S:236** — An open exit on a series’ final-bar date can receive a termination haircut associated with the later close, after the position has exited.
22. **S:236** — A close entry on the final-bar date can receive an immediate terminal loss without any modeled post-entry holding interval.
23. **S:227,237** — Scheduled exits and termination on the same session disagree about exit costs. No precedence distinguishes ordinary liquidation from terminal recovery.
24. **S:236** — Applying f to an already cost-adjusted return scales away entry costs; at f = 0, the haircut erases their separate economic effect.
25. **S:225,236** — Applying f to the dividend-inclusive factor also haircuts dividend cash. Previously detached cash and residual security value are not separated.
26. **S:237** — Censoring says “marked at its last close,” potentially overriding a declared open exit on the archive’s final session.
27. **S:237** — After every entered position terminates, subsequent cohort means are undefined; the “no entered position” idle rule does not clearly cover this state.
28. **S:241** — Invalid position losses below −100% can be diluted by other names or slots and escape the book-level ruin check.
29. **S:242** — The ruin threshold refuses positive residual wealth up to 10⁻⁹; it is a near-ruin policy, not detection of actual ruin.
30. **S:224,241; r6_monthly_trial.py:418** — Computing returns by subtracting one can round a positive gross factor to −1. The reused substrate explicitly preserves factors to avoid this.
31. **S:110,226** — Splits, dividends, and a terminal fraction do not specify treatment of merger consideration, spin-offs, rights, or other non-dividend distributions.
32. **S:220,375** — Equal omission of rebalance costs is not equal economic bias: arm and control can have very different turnover. The simplification is not necessarily conservative.
33. **S:211** — A split with no calendar-usable formation has no first entry or last exit; T, the stored grid, and its refusal are undefined.
34. **S:74,211** — No rule requires the holdout end to be covered by the archive. Future or incomplete sessions can become idle observations or false terminations.
35. **S:211** — “Usable formations” must mean calendar usability only; filtering by eligibility, scores, or successful entries would make T price-dependent despite the stated invariant.
36. **S:217,262** — Idle dates count fully toward T and the short-sample floor, allowing calendar length to substitute for dates carrying actual exposure.
37. **S:217,231** — Zero-return cash is assumed despite different idle exposure between books. Its effect on active performance is not necessarily conservative.
38. **S:140** — Storing only canonical active returns does not preserve the arm/control returns, memberships, weights, or cash flows needed to audit ruin and return construction.
39. **S:257** — A 2h lag floor does not cover dependence introduced by gaps longer than h, repeated signals, or slow-moving common exposures.
40. **S:260** — Adjacent L-session blocks are not necessarily independent, especially when the estimator includes covariance at lag L. The block count does not establish Student-t degrees of freedom.
41. **S:261** — Larger tails than a normal distribution do not establish conservatism relative to the actual finite-sample HAC statistic.
42. **S:260; r6_monthly_trial.py:379** — The claimed cancellation fix fails: the shared lower-tail implementation subtracts from 0.5 internally. Reproduction gives `cdf(-10,100) = 0` and `cdf(-100,100) ≈ −1.11e−16`.
43. **S:264** — Negative finite p-values are not covered by the non-finite refusal rule and can enter BY as impossible, exceptionally significant values.
44. **S:301** — Block-df p-values do not affect validation’s t > 3 gate. The advertised finite-sample adjustment protects only the discovery screen.
45. **S:263** — “Non-empty arm” is determined before entry; 30 qualifying formations can contain no successfully entered arm positions.
46. **S:263** — Counting formations at least h sessions apart lacks a specified selection algorithm. Different subsets can produce different refusal decisions.
47. **S:263** — Thirty disjoint formation windows need not provide 30 independent observations when the same names, persistent signals, or gap-spanning returns recur.
48. **S:264** — Non-finite protection covers shared statistics functions, but not expressly position arithmetic, cohort means, own HAC calculations, or serialization.
49. **S:143,265** — “Every exception” from shared statistics becomes a permanent statistical refusal, conflicting with retryable infrastructure errors when those errors surface inside a shared call.
50. **S:269** — Effective sample size for a mean does not generally correct Sharpe-ratio uncertainty; dependence in squared returns and mean–variance cross-moments is omitted.
51. **S:272** — V[SR] excludes abandoned or incomplete discovery outcomes. Selectively completing trials can narrow V while M still counts the discarded searches.
52. **S:274** — Nonzero variance alone admits Sharpes from economically invalid or ruined canonical series whose other statistics refused.
53. **S:272,382** — Mixing different lengths and activity rates makes V partly measure unequal estimation noise, rather than a common trial-Sharpe population.
54. **S:272,278** — V comes from one hunt, while M represents all hunts and inherited searches. Increasing M does not repair an unrepresentative or understated V.
55. **S:253,268** — Canonical base-cell V is reused for other dividend, termination, and cost populations without establishing comparable Sharpe dispersion.
56. **S:274** — Ten measured trials need not be ten distinct return series. Clones can satisfy the population floor without adding information.
