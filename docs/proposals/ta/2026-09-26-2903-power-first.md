## Rule applied
Queue rule 1: power at the smallest economically worthwhile net edge must be **shown** ≥ 0.5 at the bar before an arm
is built or frozen; otherwise redesign or drop, with the reason recorded. No bar is lowered.

- **Declared edge: 1.5%/yr net**, arm minus its own 1/N control: the rule's own example for a long-only selection
  arm, as in #2902. It is not sized to pass or fail: the NI verdict below holds for any declared edge up to the
  minimum detectable edge (4.5–5.4%/yr).
- **Bar: t > 3**, the programme floor; the family-deflated bar is higher, so power at the real bar is no higher than
  shown. Power is Φ(edge × √years / TE − t), IID normal approximation (limits as in #2902's note).

## Leg 2: opportunistic insider net buying — power not shown
The programme's construction (a periodic ranking on strictly backward insider history, held for the selection
period) has **no pre-window estimate of edge or TE in this note**, so its power is not shown and, under the rule, it
is not built. The programme row already calls it "a different, untested claim". Its window could be longer than
leg 1's: the DERA insider ZIPs start 2006 (programme lines 34–36), ~18.2 years to 2024-08 → IR 0.70 for 50% power,
i.e. TE ≤ 2.1% at the declared edge. An insider ranking that touches a small subset of names each period would need
to track its 1/N control that closely.

What the literature does give is CMP's **monthly event** portfolio (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski, *JF* 67(3) 2012; NBER
w16454, Table IV, 1989–2007: a firm is held in month t+1 if any insider made an opportunistic buy in month t; a buy
enters regardless of offsetting sales, so it is not a net-buying rank). Its long-only buy leg, DGTW
characteristic-adjusted:

| weighting | monthly | t | IR (t / √19) | implied annual TE of DGTW-adjusted return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| equal | 1.24% | 4.99 | 1.14 | 13.0% |
| value | 0.57% | 2.35 | 0.54 | 12.7% |

(t / √years equals the annualised IR when t is the monthly mean over its IID standard error; Table IV's DGTW row is a
mean of characteristic-adjusted returns. The TE is against DGTW's 125 benchmarks, not a 1/N control.)

- At the declared 1.5%/yr, IR ≈ 0.12: power < 0.01 on any window above.
- Cost on the programme's frozen basis (0.725% half-spread per traded dollar, the #2908 basis = 1.45% round trip):
  if the book turns over fully each month, 17.4%/yr exceeds the EW leg's gross 14.9%/yr. CMP do not report turnover;
  the leg is net-positive only below ~10 full round trips a year. On the cheaper `cost_model.BANDS` sensitivity (≥ $100 0.322,
  $20–100 0.509, $5–20 0.571 round trip) net IR is 0.85 / 0.67 / 0.62 at 12 round trips — 0.85 would clear 0.83 at 13
  years, but only at the large-cap band for an equal-weighted, small-cap-heavy book, and still with power < 0.01 at
  the declared edge.
- This monthly construction is the event-form insider trade the operator cut on 2026-08-22. That excludes it as an
  implementation; it is not evidence against the periodic ranking, whose question stays open and unmeasured.

## Verdict: not run as specified
- Leg 1 (net issuance) fails queue rule 1 at the declared edge (power ≤ 0.023), and its minimum detectable edge is
  above its own gross pre-window premium.
- Leg 2 (insider periodic ranking) has no shown power; the rule requires it shown before a build.
- **#2903 closes unrun.** No declaration is written and no register row is charged, on the same basis as #2902: the
  step reads published pre-window portfolios and aggregate row counts, never an intended-window or holdout arm return.

What would reopen it, at the declared edge (or a larger one justified on economic grounds before the step): a
pre-window estimate showing power ≥ 0.5 at t > 3 for a construction the programme admits — e.g. a periodic insider
net-buying rank with TE ≤ ~2.1% over the 2006+ window, or a repurchaser tilt whose minimum detectable edge on an
admissible window falls to its own estimated premium net of the frozen cost basis.
