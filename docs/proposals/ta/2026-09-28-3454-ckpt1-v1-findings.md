# #3454 ckpt-1 v1 findings (Codex, 2026-09-28)

Verbatim from `codex exec` on spec v1 (e8668cec). Disposition in the spec's Status section.

Line references below refer to the [hunt 3 proposal](docs/proposals/ta/2026-09-28-3454-hunt-3-spec.md).

1. **Framing—not acceptable as written (L20–67).** Removing exactly the two failed gates is result-dependent selection. Disclosure makes that transparent; it does not restore confirmatory validity. An explicitly amended exploratory continuation followed by a genuinely independent, frozen test can be honest. Re-registration alone cannot.

2. **Deviation 2 exceeds the explicit ruling (L27–39).** R1 removes the stress gate; nothing explicitly removes power-first. R2 creates an ambiguity, not a documented repeal. Obtain an explicit amendment; otherwise condition 4 still closes hunt 3 before validation.

3. **Infeasibility is not a statistical defect (L35–39).** High tracking error legitimately makes an 8% excess difficult to distinguish from zero. “No candidate is expected to pass” does not justify removing the requirement—and is not evidence that no candidate can pass.

4. **Universal stress-gate claim is unsupported (L21–25).** Failure depends on turnover, holding period and gross return. One construction cannot establish that the gate “would have failed any hunt.”

5. **FLAG—sample presented as population assurance (L21–25).** Means and p90s from a calm, unconditional census neither bound individual spreads nor establish costs at historical post-loss opens. Being above those summaries does not prove the stress charge unrealistic.

6. **“Unseen by every hunt” overstates cleanliness (L66–67).** The predecessor explicitly discloses overlapping #2481 variants and inspection of validation SPY returns. Carry that disclosure forward and audit exposure before calling validation independent. If independence cannot be supported, use untouched data or close the confirmatory claim.

7. **PASS does not demonstrate positive excess over SPY (L50–54).** Active significance tests arm minus control. An arm can significantly beat a losing control without significantly beating SPY. Forward cost measurement supplies no missing return evidence.

8. **Power probabilities are conditional, not validation guarantees (L41–51).** The arithmetic is correct, but assumes normality and transported discovery variance. Canonical-cell variance does not establish power for the worst base cell, DSR or the joint rule; identical discovery policies need not remain identical.

9. **Inherited biases remain binding (L175–181).** Survivorship, stale exits, terminal assumptions and uncharged rebalancing can manufacture both apparent excess and headroom. Reporting them does not make the resulting capital threshold conservative.

10. **Inheritance itself is sensible; the proposed implementation is incomplete (L75–89).** Reusing immutable evidence avoids a duplicate look. But `declaration_numbers` constructs V[SR] only from outcomes whose `hunt_id` equals hunt 3. Budget zero leaves that population empty; the predecessor explicitly requires zero eligible trials to refuse. Changing the pin lookup alone cannot freeze validation.

11. **Inheritance needs a complete provenance contract (L77–79).** Require the source row’s owner, discovery split, evaluate purpose, usable status, candidate hash and full outcome digest; reject missing, pending or incompatible rows. Closure membership and candidate equality alone are insufficient.

12. **BY charging is bookkeeping, not a cure (L85–87).** Adding one genuine inherited search can tighten the denominator. It cannot make an adaptively selected discovery p-value valid. BY’s dependence correction still requires valid null p-values. [Benjamini–Yekutieli](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~ybenja/depApr27.pdf)

13. **The exact M transition is unspecified (L85–89).** Demonstrate r16 contributes exactly once alongside existing discovery rows, reserved validation pins and the eventual validation row. Keep the inherited discovery p-value once; duplicating it can change ranks and rejections.

14. **M+1 has practically no effect on this flag.** The predecessor reports p underflowing to zero, which remains flagged under any finite M. At fixed rank, 379→380 tightens BY’s cutoff only about 0.30%; do not imply this meaningfully penalises the gate relaxation.

15. **Re-registration has no terminal invariant (L76–84, L121).** Budget zero prevents discovery rows, not future aliases or further gate amendments. Freeze a lineage-level disposition after validation failure; otherwise hunt 4 can repeat the same escape.

16. **Unchanged model ID does not freeze the changed decision procedure (L80–84).** Keeping gate logic outside hashed modules preserves computation identity, not inferential identity. Version and verify inheritance, gate semantics and their dependencies separately.

17. **Reported conditions can accidentally remain gates (L98–104).** “All must hold” and “a refused cell … counts as not met” include reported stress/power outputs. Specify whether unavailable reported outputs block; distinguish unavailable data from a finite reported failure.

18. **Flag-drift inheritance contradicts deviation 2 (L116–117).** The predecessor closes when condition 4 no longer holds. Importing that rule unchanged reintroduces the dropped power gate.

19. **Cell and finiteness validation need strengthening (L98–113).** Require the exact six base and six stress keys, valid statuses and finite values. The supplied runner uses relative cell counts and comparisons that can accept positive infinity; its stated contract exceeds its checks.

20. **Runner hashing is not runtime enforcement (L109–114).** Specify verification at execution and promotion, including helper dependencies. Every promotion path must enforce the frozen gate; a script-only check leaves generic harness PASS insufficient.

21. **Gate-blocker removal needs an invariant (L113–114).** “Until the block exists” is ambiguous between implementation availability and a valid declaration instance. Missing, malformed or mismatched blocks must continue refusing after the global awaiting marker is removed.

22. **H has a unit ambiguity (L127–130).** The formula returns a decimal return: **0.00176587**, equivalent to **0.176587 percentage points**. Calling the formula’s output “percentage points” without an explicit ×100 risks a 100-fold implementation error.

23. **H is not a proved safe charge (L127–133).** Its derivation is approximate, but capital treats it as binding headroom. Slot compounding, return-dependent costs and daily rebalancing need an exact sensitivity calculation or a conservative error bound.

24. **The 50.2 slope check does not validate cost sensitivity (L132–133).** From the supplied outcome, 50.2 is reproduced by dividing the annualised return difference by the per-trade **net-return** difference. That is an annualisation comparison, not an independently measured derivative with respect to spread charges.

25. **Utilisation is not turnover (L128–129).** Exposure time does not uniquely determine entry/exit charges when entries fail, positions terminate early or cash remains. Define the charge-bearing turnover measure and use the same tracker-covered sessions as `excess_ann`.

26. **One u need not serve every cell (L127–136).** Different termination policies can produce different utilisation and cost sensitivity in validation. Taking minimum excess first and dividing by one utilisation value need not equal minimum cell-specific headroom.

27. **Forward averaging does not match portfolio weighting (L147–159).** A pooled position mean overweights larger cohorts. The historical book weights slots equally and positions within slots; ties, missing quotes and changing cohort sizes make the distinction material.

28. **H_val freeze chronology is contradictory (L135, L189–191).** H_val cannot be numerically frozen before validation produces it. If “before the look” means before forward observation, parallel forward delivery needs an explicit sequencing rule; alternatively freeze the formula first and specify when the value becomes immutable.

29. **min(H,H_val) is no confidence bound (L135–137).** Both margins are noisy estimates; H_val is also conditioned on validation passing. Their minimum does not establish that true remaining excess stays above 8%.

30. **Headroom edge cases are absent.** Define zero/negative/non-finite margins, zero utilisation, invalid cells and equality at the 8% bar. At H_val=0, a positive-cost trial has no positive margin; negative excess costs require explicit interpretation.

31. **The quoted cost is only a first-order approximation (L147–149).** With midpoint gross return \(R\) and relative half-spreads \(s_e,s_x\), executable gross return is \(R(1-s_x)/(1+s_e)\), before fees/dividends. Simply adding half-spreads is not exact entry-capital-normalised round-trip drag.

32. **Tariff cancellation is assumed, not established (L148–149).** It requires matching instrument, order size, notional convention and fee schedule. Minimum fees, financing, currency conversion or different execution products would not disappear.

33. **Base-band reference differs from the backtest (L148).** Forward uses entry midpoint; historical costs use the as-traded entry price. Crossing a band boundary can change \(b_i\), so this is not automatically the same cost increment used to construct H.

34. **09:31/15:55 changes the execution target (L145–146).** The backtest enters the printed open and exits the close. These quotes cannot establish costs at those prices or capture the intervening price movement.

35. **FLAG—uncited execution windows (L145–146).** Explain the operational basis for 09:31 and 15:55 and the benchmark they measure. They are bespoke windows; no cited execution-cost formulation connects them to the claimed next-open realised cost.

36. **“First capture at or after” is unbounded (L145).** It permits arbitrarily late quotes. Specify capture deadlines, source timestamps, maximum age, bid/ask synchronisation and deterministic handling of delayed responses.

37. **Calendar edge cases are missing (L145–152).** Define t+k as exchange sessions, daylight-saving handling, early closes, unscheduled closures and halted securities. A 15:55 exit does not exist on a normal half-day.

38. **Forward formation timing is underspecified (L139–146).** Freeze when completed daily bars become available, signal cutoffs and late-data handling. Corrected `price_daily` rows must not retrospectively alter selected cohorts.

39. **The full eligible population needs an audit trail (L139–142).** Freeze universe membership, broker instrument mapping, tradability and corporate-action handling. Unquotable selected names must remain in the denominator rather than disappear through broker availability filters.

40. **“Post-loss” is not guaranteed (L14, L139–142).** The predecessor explicitly notes relative selection need not select actual losses. Record that distinction; silently requiring negative returns would change the construction.

41. **The forward evidence is insufficiently pinned.** Freeze signal/version, cost-band schedule, cohort membership and relevant input identities. Append-only quote observations alone do not make the experiment reproducible.

42. **FLAG—10% missing is an unsupported tolerance (L150–151).** No rationale or missing-data sensitivity analysis is supplied. There is no universal missing percentage that makes complete-case inference valid.

43. **Missingness is likely informative (L150–151).** Halts and absent quotes plausibly identify the most expensive or unexecutable positions. Even below 10%, excluding them can reverse PASS. Bound their contribution or refuse when the verdict is not robust.

44. **Missing denominator is undefined.** Specify positions versus legs versus notional, whether one absent leg invalidates the entire position, and whether limits apply overall or per session/band. Overall 10% can conceal complete loss of a difficult subgroup.

45. **Quote validity is incomplete.** Cover negative/non-finite prices, locked quotes, stale indicative quotes, zero depth, suspended instruments and symbol mismatches. Define whether valid extreme spreads remain observations rather than becoming discretionary “bad data.”

46. **Endpoint anchoring permits selection (L152).** Freeze the exact first formation/entry session and dates, including intraday freezes and operational delays. Otherwise choosing when to freeze or restart selects the measured market regime.

47. **Endpoint completion is ambiguous (L152).** Twenty entry sessions require later exits. Define finalisation deadlines, late arrivals, permanently missing exits and amendment policy without extending the sampling window.

48. **Entry-session clustering misses cross-cluster dependence (L153–154).** Names recur, holding periods overlap, market conditions persist, and one cohort’s exit day can be another’s entry day. Entry clusters cannot simply be assumed independent.

49. **SE_cluster is not an executable specification (L155).** State estimator, weights, finite-sample correction and degrees of freedom. Include empty clusters, unequal sizes, singleton observations, zero estimated variance and too few usable clusters.

50. **FLAG—1.645 is unsupported with 20 estimated clusters (L155).** Published small-cluster methods exist; specify and cite one. Even a simple independent-cluster t reference gives approximately 1.729 with 19 degrees of freedom, and may still be inadequate. [Cameron–Miller](https://www.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/cameron/research/Cameron_Miller_JHR_2014_July_09.pdf), [Pustejovsky–Tipton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01981)

51. **FLAG—20 sessions lacks a power-derived justification (L152–160).** Freeze a desired detectable gap, error rate and power, then derive duration using the chosen dependence model. Published one-sided sample-size formulations exist; the spec cites none. [NIST](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc222.htm)

52. **Design inputs are unvalidated (L157–160).** Two evening snapshots do not establish the variance of paired entry-plus-exit excess costs or session ICC=0.1. The 6.8 design effect assumes equal cluster sizes and that dependence model; neither is demonstrated.

53. **Power arithmetic is loosely mixed, not substantially wrong (L159–160).** SD 0.75–1.0 implies SE **0.0569–0.0759**. At H=0.18, normal-approximation power is **0.935–0.766**; using H=0.176587 gives **0.927–0.752**. State which inputs produce the claimed 0.75.

54. **Power uses the wrong threshold when H_val binds (L159–160).** It must use min(H,H_val). As that approaches zero, power at true excess cost zero approaches the test’s false-pass rate, not 75%.

55. **Reported MDE needs a definition (L156).** This is a threshold test: the relevant detectable quantity is the gap between allowed cost and true cost. Specify power, reference distribution and dependence assumptions; realised-SE MDE is not achieved power.

56. **FAIL and PASS have asymmetric evidential meanings (L155).** PASS requires an upper confidence bound; FAIL needs only a point estimate. That can be an operational rule, but FAIL does not establish true cost exceeds the threshold. Equality also differs from R4’s “≤”.

57. **FLAG—forward sample is not a population safety guarantee.** Twenty sessions estimate costs for a particular regime and size. They do not certify all future post-loss names, stressed sessions or execution sizes; declare the population and limits of the claim.

58. **F2’s “≥30” leaves optional stopping open (L165).** No exact endpoint, maximum duration or retry rule is frozen. Thirty trips on one day also provide little independent information. **FLAG:** the sample threshold has no cited power or precision basis.

59. **F2 subsample selection is unspecified (L165).** Declare selection probabilities, timing, strata and weights before observing outcomes. Convenient liquid names cannot certify the full selected population.

60. **F2 does not control combined uncertainty (L166–167).** Comparing mean slippage with min(H,H_val)−mean quote excess discards quote uncertainty and adds no slippage uncertainty. A joint upper bound on total excess execution cost—or a justified allocation of error—is needed.

61. **“Same-moment quote” and slippage are undefined (L165–166).** Specify decision/arrival/fill timestamps, executable bid/ask benchmark, signed entry/exit aggregation, units and weights. Midpoint-based slippage can double-count spread already included in c_i.

62. **F2 execution failures need rules.** Cover rejected orders, partial fills, delayed exits, halts, non-fills and broker netting of repeated names. Excluding unsuccessful round trips produces completion bias.

63. **FLAG—demo subsample cannot certify live fills (L164–172).** Demo execution may not reproduce queueing, depth, impact or live rejection behaviour. State what the sandbox establishes and what remains unmeasured; do not turn the demo sample into a live-capital assurance.

64. **Funding amount is statistically material (L171–172).** Fill quality and impact depend on order size. Asking the amount only after cost qualification leaves the eventual funded size outside the tested protocol unless a validated sizing limit is already frozen.

65. **Forward/F2 terminal dispositions are missing.** Define closure after FAIL or undetermined, whether reruns are allowed, how additional trials are charged, and when results expire. “No extension” for one quote trial does not prevent repeated trials until one passes.
