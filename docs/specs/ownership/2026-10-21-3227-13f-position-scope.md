# #3227 — a manager's 13F figure is counted once when its own joint Schedule 13D/G reports the same block for a co-filer

Status: spec (2026-10-21), revision 3. It builds on #3897/#3898 (co-filer links widened, merged `197be10d`), whose
proposition 3 keeps every identity with a 13F kept row out of the co-filer graph. Research: the 2026-10-21 15:19Z
comment on #3227.

Rung: a read-path metric change, which is the corpus rung (full-population A/B). This document is the judgement artefact
(Codex checkpoint 1, 3 rounds, the cap; round 3's findings are fixed here without a fourth round, as #3894's
and #3897's were).

## Revision history

- **Revision 1** admitted a 13F identity into the co-filer graph when its own 13D/G cover reported dispositive =
  aggregate = its 13F count, with a legal "proof" that the 13F set is the manager's whole beneficial block, plus twin
  edges. **Round 1, 4 P1s, all accepted:** investment discretion (§3(a)(35)) covers authority over purchases *or*
  sales, so it does not entail dispositive power over every counted security; a cover dated before the 13F period end
  does not show the same block at that date (a block can be sold and an equal one bought); a joint filing does not put
  a co-filer's whole kept total inside the manager's block; inherited twin edges had no filing behind them.
- **Revision 2** withdrew the proof and the graph change, and identified a duplicate by exact non-round equality across
  four figures, with a null test to set a roundness gate. **Round 2, 2 P1s, accepted:** equal counts do not identify
  sets (a count-preserving substitution is a structural defeat, not a coincidence), and the null test measured where
  equalities occur, not the rule's false-removal rate, and its reading was by name. Its graph-preservation argument
  held on validated input (finding 7).
- **Revision 3** (this) does three things:
  - it requires a **structural link in one document**: the co-filer is itself a reporting person on the manager's own
    cover filing, reporting the same figures there;
  - it drops the roundness gate and the null test, because nothing here rests on coincidence statistics any more;
  - it **declares the remaining identification risk as accepted policy**, as #3894 declared MAX, with every case and
    its dates listed for review. Round 2's P2/P3 findings are answered in place (§ Round 2 disposition).
- **Round 3** accepted policy declaration as a legitimate resolution. Its one P1 was a sentence that still claimed the
  two blocks on C are one block, and a missing risk (M and k holding apart, or partly apart, on the same statement).
  Both are fixed below, with its P2/P3s (§ Round 3 disposition).

## Problem

#3897 left 53 two-member cliques on 52 instruments unfolded (−760,924,577 in its revision-3 census). In each, a
co-filer k and an `institutions` identity M carry the same count. M's kept figure is its 13F count; k's is a Form 3/4
or 13D/G figure. The pie adds the count twice.

Example: CVI.US. Carl C. Icahn's (CIK 921669) 13F for 2026-06-30 reports 71,201,875 shares. His Schedule 13D/A
`0001193125-26-067602` (filed 2026-02-24) is a joint statement of three reporting persons. Each reports sole
dispositive 0, shared dispositive 71,201,875 and aggregate 71,201,875: Icahn Enterprises Holdings L.P. (1034563), Icahn
Enterprises G.P. Inc. (1257324) and Icahn himself. 1257324's kept `insiders` figure is 71,201,875. The pie counts
142,403,750 for the two, against 100,530,599 outstanding.

## Source rule

- **Rule 13d-1(k)(1)** (17 CFR 240.13d-1, as quoted in #3897): *"Whenever two or more persons are required to file a
  statement containing the information required by Schedule 13D or Schedule 13G with respect to the same securities,
  only one statement need be filed"*. A joint cover filing is one statement about one class of securities.
- **Schedule 13D/13G cover page**, one block per reporting person (`soleDispositivePower`, `sharedDispositivePower`,
  `aggregateAmountOwned`; `data-sources/sec-edgar.md` §2.4). Under **Rule 13d-3(a)** the aggregate is beneficial
  ownership through voting and/or investment power, and the dispositive rows are investment power.
- **Rule 13f-1(a)(1), (b)** and **Exchange Act §3(a)(35)**: a 13F count is investment discretion (round 1: it does
  not entail dispositive power over each counted share).

**No rule identifies a 13F position with a beneficial-ownership position**, and round 1 established that the
definitions do not entail it. The link this revision uses is the strongest the stored data has: on one joint statement
about the same securities, the manager and the co-filer each report the identical dispositive and aggregate figure, and
that figure is also the manager's 13F count and the co-filer's kept figure. Treating the 13F figure as that same block is
a **declared policy**, fixed by construction and frozen here. Its residual risk is stated below, not measured.

## Rule (frozen)

**Candidate M.** An `institutions` identity with a canonical CIK, total T > 0, every kept row a 13F row, and every
kept row carrying the same non-NULL `as_of_date` P (a `{P, NULL}` mix is not a candidate). This is checked before M takes part in anything else.

**Cover C.** M's own `blockholder_filings` row for this instrument (canonical `reporter_cik` = M's canonical CIK),
the latest by `(filed_at, accession_number)` among rows with `filed_at < P + 1 day`. The comparison is in UTC, on the
full timestamp. C **matches** when both dispositive fields are non-NULL, sole + shared = T, and aggregate = T.

Canonical CIKs are `_canonical_cik`'s output and must be all digits on both sides; anything else never matches.

**Twin k.** A #3898 node (`_cofiler_graph`'s node set) whose canonical CIK differs from M's, that is a reporting
person on C's accession for this instrument, and that meets all three:

- its row there has both dispositive fields non-NULL;
- that row's dispositive sum and aggregate both equal T;
- k's own total equals T.

**M is a duplicate of k** when C matches, M has exactly one twin k, and k is no other **cover-matching** candidate's
twin. Uniqueness is counted over candidates whose own C matches; a candidate whose C fails is not a duplicate of
anything and does not compete.

**The policy assumption.** C is a single statement *"with respect to the same securities"* in which M and k each
report investment power over, and beneficial ownership of, exactly T shares, and M's 13F count at P and k's kept figure
are also T. **The policy treats M's 13F figure as the block k's figure already counts.** Nothing we extract proves it.
#3898's own docstring accepts that co-filers can hold apart, and the same is possible on one statement.

**Accepted identification risk (declared, not measured).** The fold removes more shares than actually overlap when any
of these holds:

1. **M and k hold apart, or partly apart, on C itself**: each reports T shares of the class on one joint statement,
   but the two sets of T are distinct or only partly shared.
2. **M's 13F count at P is not C's block for M**, through count-preserving turnover between C's date and P, or
   through discretion over shares outside M's investment power that happens to total exactly T.
3. **k's kept figure is not k's block on C**, through turnover between the two filings, or because the kept figure has
   a different reporting scope (another form, other lines) that happens to total T.
4. **Class scope.** C's class is not the class of M's 13F rows or of k's rows. The instrument mapping makes them the
   same instrument; the class is not separately verified (#3897 states the same).

**What this is accepted against.** Today the pie counts T twice whenever M's 13F is the block k's figure counts. The
policy expects that to be the usual case for a manager whose own joint 13D/G reports the block, and that is an
expectation, not a measurement. Compared with #3898's co-filing evidence, the rule adds an exact equality across four
reported figures and a joint statement on which both M and k report them. It does not require k's kept row to sit on
that statement (GETY and OLMA do not), and it accepts the cross-source and cross-date assumptions above, which #3898
does not make. Every case is listed with all its contributing rows and dates for review (Acceptance), so a case where
a gap makes risk 2 or 3 plausible can be read, not inferred.

**Dates.** `blockholder_filings.date_of_event` is NULL on all 122,134 rows (#3948, filed with this research), so C is
chosen by filing availability. That selects which statement to compare. It does not claim the block is unchanged at P;
risk 2 above says so.

## Change (read path only)

### 1. The cover read

Between `_read_cofilers` and `_reconcile_cofiler_groups`, when any candidate exists, the caller reads this
instrument's `blockholder_filings` rows with `filed_at < max(P) + 1 day` (UTC). Columns: `reporter_cik`,
`accession_number`, `filed_at`, both dispositive fields and the aggregate. `idx_blockholder_filings_instrument_filed_at`
serves the read, and it is bounded by instrument and date. Python canonicalises `reporter_cik` with `_canonical_cik`,
the function the co-filer map and the identity keys use, before any comparison, so no SQL trim decides a match. A
row whose canonical CIK is not all digits is dropped from the comparison. No candidate, no query.

### 2. `_fold_13f_duplicates` (new, pure)

Runs on owner-once's output. It runs **after** the per-identity category check that `_cofiler_graph` does today (lines
4459–4469), which moves into a shared helper both call, so a breach still raises on the original input. It runs before
`_reconcile_cofiler_groups`, with the cover rows.

For each duplicate M (the rule above):

- it removes M's rows from `institutions`;
- it appends them, then each row's own dropped entries, to the dropped sources of k's largest row (largest `shares`,
  then the lowest `winning_accession`, then input order), with #3898's de-duplication key
  `(filer_cik, filer_name, source, accession_number, shares)`;
- it emits one correction:
  - kind `cofiler_group_collapse`;
  - `filer_name`/`filer_cik` k's, `shares_removed` T, `source_channel` `13f`, `winning_source`/`winning_accession`
    k's largest row's;
  - detail: *"13F duplicate counted once (#3227): {M} ({M CIK}) 13F {T} for {P}; on joint Schedule 13D/G {C accession}
    (filed {date}) {M} and {k} each report dispositive power and aggregate amount {T}. Counted once by policy: overlap
    on that statement, across dates and in class scope is not verified."*

k keeps its rows and category through this fold. #3898's pass then runs unchanged on the result. If k loses a #3898
clique, M's entries travel with k's onto that clique's keeper by #3898's existing union; the 13F correction still
names k, the identity the 13F figure was folded onto.

**Correction consumers (audited 2026-10-21):**

- `app/api/instruments.py` serialises the list as is (~5522–5539) and counts `suppressed_by_13f_nt` only;
- the CSV export (`ownership_rollup.py` ~7042) writes one row per correction;
- `frontend/src/api/ownership.ts` declares the type; no frontend component reads `corrections_applied`
  (`grep -rn corrections frontend/src` finds the type declaration and two unrelated comments).

So two `cofiler_group_collapse` corrections with one `filer_cik` (k keeps a clique and absorbs a 13F duplicate) are
both kept and both exported. A regression test pins that through API serialisation and the CSV export, not only the
pure fold.

**Guarantee, on validated input** (round 2, finding 7). M is not in #3898's node sets: the widened set excludes 13F
rows, and the base set excludes `institutions`. Nodes, the canonical-CIK node indexes, edges, the base-node subset test
and the ordered collapse list are built from nodes only. So removing M's rows after the category check leaves all of
them unchanged, and this pie = #3898's pie − Σ T on the same input. The `rows_by_identity`, `totals` and `canon` maps
do lose M, which nothing in the pass reads for a non-node. The A/B asserts the edges and the collapse list unchanged per
instrument.

### 3. Docstrings and comments

`_COFILER_CATEGORIES`, `_COFILER_EXCLUDED_SOURCES` and `_reconcile_cofiler_groups` say that a 13F identity is not a
node, and that `_fold_13f_duplicates` folds the one case this spec defines.
