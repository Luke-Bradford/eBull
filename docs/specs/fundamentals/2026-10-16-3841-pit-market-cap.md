# #3841: point-in-time market cap for the live tradable universe

**Ticket:** #3841 (refs #3822, #3740). **Status:** spec v3.1. Codex checkpoint 1 ran three rounds, the operator's cap.
v3.1 is the post-round-3 revision. The disposition of every open round-3 finding is in "Checkpoint record" at the
end; the reviews' raw outputs are not committed.
- Round 1 rejected v1, which inferred splits from share-count jumps. It raised 42 findings; the round-2 file carried
  34 of them.
- Round 2 (53 findings) worked on v2's primitive, the observed price basis. It kept objections on 13F mapping and
  vendor adjustment semantics, and found the side-selection rule unidentifiable, the bands overlapping and the Form 4
  gate's formula wrong.
- Round 3 (40 findings) worked on v3.

v3 drops side selection, so a count near a split is refused rather than guessed. v3.1 fixes the round-3 defects and
states the rest as limits.

**Consumers it unlocks:** value-weighted books (#3740's VW construction), size filters, cap-relative sector and theme
limits, and a cap-weighted control for the momentum scorecard (#3822).

**Output definition.** For instrument i and session S, the output is the selected share count public before S, put on
the share basis of the stored close, × that close. Like CRSP/JKP `ME` it is an estimate. It equals actual
capitalisation when four conditions hold:
- the count is correct and covers the quoted class;
- shares changed between the count's date and S only by the splits the factor corrects;
- the factor is exact;
- the close is correct.

The acceptance gates below measure how far those conditions fail.

## Problem

Figures in this section are printed by `PYTHONPATH=. uv run python scripts/measure_3841_premise.py` (dev DB,
2026-10-16).

- `instrument_valuation.market_cap_live` is one undated value per instrument.
- **Price basis.** eToro `price_daily` is back-adjusted for splits up to its last refetch. NFLX reads 110–115 either
  side of its 10-for-1 split in November 2025, ORLY 90–92 either side of its 15-for-1, FAST 40–41 either side of its
  2-for-1. XBRL counts are as reported (NFLX's cover count goes from 423.7M to 4.22B), so `count × close` is wrong by
  every split after the count's date.
- **No split register exists, and none can be inferred safely.**
  - `price_adjustments` is empty.
  - eToro's API has no corporate-action field (live portal, 2026-10-16).
  - Yahoo's ToS §2.4(i) bars automated collection (`research-price-corpus.md`).
  - A count jump with the XBRL ratio fact cannot separate a split from a merger (round 1).

## The primitive: a raw price observed in SEC filings

A 13F-HR reports, per holding, shares (Column 5) and market value (Column 4). Form 13F Special Instruction 8 (SEC 1685,
07-23): "use the value at the close of trading on the last trading day of the calendar year or quarter … Enter values
rounded to the nearest dollar". Values are in dollars since 2023-01-03 (`sec-edgar.md` §7.1). PRN rows are dropped at
ingest (§7.2, `thirteen_f_normalise.py:81`).

Value ÷ shares is therefore a **reported raw quarter-end close**, an estimate of it subject to rounding and filer error,
on that date's share basis. Its ratio to the stored close on the same session measures eToro's re-basing of that
session since then:

F̂(q) = median over distinct 13F filers of (value ÷ shares) ÷ the stored close on the last trading session ≤ q.

**What the premise script observes.** These are observations, not a vendor contract (see "Checkpoint record"):
- **Splits and reverse splits.** The named series move F̂ by the split ratio, in the right direction, and back to about
  1 after the split. NFLX reads 10.0 until 2025-09-30 and 1.002 at 2025-12-31. BKNG reads 25 until 2026-03-31, KLAC 10
  until 2026-03-31, APH 2 until 2026-06-30, and DD 0.334 (a 1-for-3) until 2026-03-31. HON reads 0.50, but on
  2–26 filers, below the anchor minimum.
- **One spin-off.** FTV reads 1.001–1.016 across its Ralliant spin-off (2025-06-28). A price back-adjusted for the
  spin would read well above 1.
- **Spread under the anchor rule below**, measured exactly as specified: ≥ 20 filers on their latest-filed report, a
  close on s(q), and the coherence test.
  - Each quarter from 2024-06-30 has between 1,506 and 3,744 anchors, of which 1 to 35 are `incoherent`.
  - Of the `ok` anchors, 92.0–96.1% have m(F̂) ≤ 1.02.
  - Between 1 and 15 per quarter are ambiguous (1.10 < m < 1.25).
  - The largest m − 1 among snapped anchors is 0.100, at the band edge.

  These observations are unlabelled, so this is a spread, not a count of non-splits.

The NFLX share counts and the API and ToS facts above come from the queries and pages cited where they appear, not
from this script.

## Construction

### Anchors

Every calendar quarter end q from 2024-06-30 gets an **observation status** for instrument i. The anchor session s(q)
is the last NYSE session on or before q, from `market_calendar.us_market_status` (`nyse-market-calendar-v1`). The
status is `ok` when all of the following hold, and otherwise the named failure:
- `no_close`: i has no stored close on exactly s(q), or that close is NULL, not finite, or ≤ 0;
- `thin`: fewer than 20 distinct filers. A filer counts if it holds a row with finite shares > 0, finite value > 0 and
  no put/call, in its **latest accession for q** (an amendment replaces the filer's earlier report for that quarter),
  and it contributes the median of those rows;
- `incoherent`: fewer than 75% of filers lie within m ≤ 1.02 of the median, where m(x) = max(x, 1/x) of the
  filer-to-median ratio. A second cluster of a quarter of the filers is enough to fail;
- `anchor_ambiguous`: 1.10 < m(F̂) < 1.25.

For an `ok` status:
- m(F̂) ≤ 1.10 gives Φ = 1;
- m(F̂) ≥ 1.25 gives Φ = F̂, an estimate (KLAC 2026-03-31 reads 10.27). Its error carries into the cap unmeasured;
  see "Checkpoint record".

A quarter whose 13F due date (45 days after q; Rule 13f-1(a)(1)) has not passed is marked `provisional`. The daily
rebuild re-reads it as filings land. The status, F̂, filer count, coherence share and accession list are kept for
every quarter, `ok` or not.

### The factor for one count

The basis interval [b_lo, b_hi] is as in the source rule, compared on the anchor's observation session s(q), not the
quarter label. q_a is the last quarter with s(q_a) ≤ b_lo. The count's window W is every quarter from q_a to the first
quarter with s(q) ≥ b_hi, or to the latest quarter if there is none.

1. **Left edge.** The q_a status must be `ok`, else `no_left_anchor`.
2. **No bridging.** Every quarter in W must be `ok`. A `thin`, `incoherent`, `no_close` or `anchor_ambiguous` quarter
   inside W refuses the count (`basis_unresolved`). It is never skipped.
3. **Agreement.** max Φ / min Φ over W must be ≤ 1.05, else `basis_unresolved`. Then F = Φ(q_a).
4. **Right edge.** If W reaches a quarter with s(q) ≥ b_hi, the count is bracketed and rules 1–3 decide it.

   Otherwise W ends at the latest quarter. That quarter must be one of the two most recent quarter ends (no older
   than about 6 months), and Φ must be 1 across all of W, else `basis_unresolved`.

   The reasoning: Φ at the latest `ok` quarter is the net re-basing eToro has applied to that bar since. Under
   complete back-adjustment, it would be ≠ 1 after any net split that eToro has applied since then.

What these rules guarantee: no publication against an *observed* discrepancy (a quarter that disagrees, is missing, or
is ambiguous) inside the count's window. They do not prove the window was split-free. "Checkpoint record" lists what
they cannot see: offsetting events, a re-basing under 10%, and incomplete back-adjustment.

### Selection and steps

At session S:
1. Admitted counts: from accessions accepted before S (New York date < S) whose form is in scope.
2. Cover candidates: eligible by age, with the latest context, then the latest acceptance.
   - Distinct values for that (context, acceptance) give `shares_ambiguous`. There is no fallback past them.
3. Balance-sheet candidates: used only when no cover candidate is eligible. Same ordering and ambiguity rule.

A step is a maximal run of sessions with the same selected result. Its `valid_from` is the first session that result
is selected, which may be later than its acceptance when a cover count expires. Intervals are half-open and never
overlap, and a test asserts both. Expiry comes from the age predicate itself, tested at month ends and in a leap year.

**Step row** (`instrument_share_count_pit`):
- the selection: `instrument_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `shares_concept`, `accession`, `context_date`,
  `accepted_at`, `shares_reported`, all kept even when refused;
- the result: `split_factor`, or NULL with `refusal`. The refusals are:
  - `no_left_anchor` · `basis_unresolved`;
  - `shares_ambiguous` · `shares_scale_conflict` · `shares_turnover_implausible` · `shares_discontinuity`;
  - `multiclass_unavailable` · `invalid_input` (a non-finite or non-positive operand, or a non-finite product);
  - `anchor_contradicted` (Gate 1) · `float_bound` (Gate 2);
- `evidence` jsonb: the window's quarter statuses (q, s(q), status, filers, coherence share, F̂, provisional,
  accessions), each check's operands and outcome, and the discontinuity reference (its accession, context and count);
- `revision_count`: the number of `price_daily_revision` rows for the instrument visible to the build's snapshot. The
  table is append-only, so a newly committed row raises the count whatever its id. `max(revision_id)` is not
  commit-ordered and is not used;
- `build_id`.

The session-dependent check (turnover) is evaluated per session, so a step also ends where its outcome changes. The
discontinuity reference is the previous step's selected count on the same concept that published a factor. With no
such count, the check is `untested` and recorded so.

**`market_cap_pit_builds`** (append-only):
- `build_id`, start, finish, status;
- `code_sha` and `rule_version` (the sha256 of the frozen constants and the refusal set);
- `submissions_sha256` and the max `institutional_holdings.filed_at` in the snapshot.

### Build and publication

`market_cap_pit_rebuild` is a daily job after `daily_candle_refresh`. It runs in this order:
1. take the job lock, so builds serialise;
2. open one REPEATABLE READ transaction, so every database input is one snapshot;
3. build and run Gates 1 and 2 against the staged steps in that snapshot;
4. replace every step and mark the build complete, in the same transaction.

A failing gate does not abort the build. It sets that step's refusal, as above. A build that raises rolls back, and
the previous build stays active.

The scope is `strategies.validated_universe`'s US-equity source with a primary SEC CIK.

**`instrument_market_cap_pit` view.** It is `price_daily` ⋈ the step valid at `price_date`. It returns identity,
`close`, the step fields, and `market_cap`, where `market_cap = close × shares_reported × split_factor` or NULL with
`reason`. `reason` is the step's refusal, or `invalid_input` when `close IS NULL OR NOT isfinite(close) OR close <= 0`,
or `basis_stale`. `basis_stale` means either:
- the instrument's current revision count differs from the step's `revision_count`;
- an unresolved `price_series_break` exists.

A session with no step gets no view row; the coverage report accounts for it.

Price writes and their revision rows commit in one transaction (`market_data.py`, #2414). The build must enumerate
every `price_daily` write path and confirm each records a revision row. A path that does not record one is a blocker
for this ticket, not a limit.

The view is a current estimate, not an append-only record. The first consumer that decides on it must copy the whole
view row (cap or refusal, the step fields, `evidence` and `build_id`) into its own decision row in one statement. No
consumer ships with this ticket.

## Acceptance

`scripts/measure_3841_acceptance.py` runs over every in-scope instrument and every NYSE session from 2024-07-01.

1. **Gate 1: price basis, against an independent raw source.**
   - **Input:** Form 4 non-derivative rows coded P or S (open-market or private purchase and sale) with a finite price
     > 0, whose security title names common stock. Rows are deduplicated by (accession, row); an amendment replaces its
     original.
   - **Trade-date factor.** For a trade on session d, take quarters q_k and q_{k+1} with s(q_k) ≤ d < s(q_{k+1}). If
     both are `ok` and agree (max/min ≤ 1.05), Φ(d) = Φ(q_k); otherwise the trade is `untested`.
     r = price ÷ (close(d) × Φ(d)).
   - **Pass:** per (instrument, q_k) with at least 3 trades on at least 2 distinct dates, the median r lies in
     [0.9, 1.1].
   - **On failure:** every step whose window W contains q_k is refused (`anchor_contradicted`).
   - **Reported:** tested, untested and failed quarters, per instrument and in total.
   - **Scope of the claim:** this gate checks the price basis between anchors only. It does not check the count. A P or
     S price is a transaction price, not the close, which the [0.9, 1.1] band allows for.
2. **Gate 2: count magnitude, one-sided.** `dei:EntityPublicFloat`, read from the latest accession for its context
   date, in USD, for the same CIK. The comparison session is the last NYSE session on or before that context date; a
   context with no step or no close is `uncomparable`.
   - **Fail:** cap < 0.9 × float. Every step covering that session is refused (`float_bound`).
   - **Untested:** an overstated cap passes this gate, and nothing here bounds it from above.
   - **Can fail a correct cap:** an issuance after the count makes a correct estimate fail. That refusal is the
     conservative choice.
3. **Coverage (reported).** Per month: the denominator (in-scope instruments × NYSE sessions). Then each outcome,
   reconciled to that denominator:
   - published;
   - each refusal;
   - each absence: no close, out-of-scope form, no eligible count.

   The formation-1 universe is also reported at the latest session. There is no floor. A consumer decides whether
   coverage suffices for its use.
4. **Dated external check (ETL evidence).** AAPL, GME, MSFT, JPM and HD around 2026-09-30, against companiesmarketcap's
   figure (company total), ±5%. A miss is recorded with its likely cause. Its chart series is weekly (Friday close,
   stamped the next Saturday 00:00 UTC) and has no 2026-09-30 point, so the check uses the bracketing Fridays,
   2026-09-25 and 2026-10-02, and prints 2026-09-30 beside them (slice 3).

**Tests** (pure; each states its expected outcome):
- the band boundaries (1.10, 1.25 and their reciprocals) and agreement as max/min;
- no left anchor;
- a `thin`, `incoherent` or `ambiguous` quarter inside W (refused, never bridged);
- an interior anchor after q_a that disagrees;
- a trailing window with Φ = 1, with Φ ≠ 1, and with a stale latest quarter;
- a coherent mixed population (11 at 100, 9 at 200: `incoherent`);
- a reverse split;
- an offsetting split pair, asserted to publish the wrong factor, as the stated limit;
- vintage selection, where a later amendment does not reach back, and amendment replacement within a quarter;
- cover expiry falling to the balance-sheet count;
- the month-end and leap-year age predicate;
- DST and weekend acceptance;
- a missing submissions page;
- each ported check, including an untested discontinuity;
- Gate 1:
  - a post-split trade on an old pre-split count is evaluated with Φ(d) and passes;
  - a skipped trade is reported `untested`;
  - one bad trade among three moves the median.
- Gate 2: an uncomparable float context.

One DB test covers the rebuild transaction, the view's `basis_stale` on a revision committed out of id order, and an
instrument with no revision rows.

## Not in scope

- Total-company caps for curated multi-class issuers.
- FPIs and ADRs, and non-USD listings.
- Sessions before 2024-07.
- Fixing 13F CUSIP mapping. CMCSA (4–26 filers), GE (2–35) and HON (2–26) have thin populations before 2026-06. Some
  quarters pass the 20-filer minimum and others fail it as `thin`, which refuses any window containing them.
