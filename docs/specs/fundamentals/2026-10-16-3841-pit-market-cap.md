# #3841: point-in-time market cap for the live tradable universe

**Ticket:** #3841 (refs #3822, #3740). **Status:** spec v3.1. Codex checkpoint 1 ran three rounds, the operator's cap.
v3.1 is the post-round-3 revision. The disposition of every open round-3 finding is in "Checkpoint record" at the
end; the reviews' raw outputs are not committed.
- Round 1 rejected v1, which inferred splits from share-count jumps. It raised 42 findings; the round-2 file carried
  34 of them.
- Round 2 (53 findings) worked on v2's primitive, the observed price basis. It kept objections on 13F mapping and
  vendor adjustment semantics, and found the side-selection rule unidentifiable, the bands overlapping and the Form 4
  gate's formula wrong.
- Round 3 (40 findings) worked on v3.

v3 drops side selection, so a count near a split is refused rather than guessed. v3.1 fixes the round-3 defects and
states the rest as limits.

**Consumers it unlocks:** value-weighted books (#3740's VW construction), size filters, cap-relative sector and theme
limits, and a cap-weighted control for the momentum scorecard (#3822).

**Output definition.** For instrument i and session S, the output is the selected share count public before S, put on
the share basis of the stored close, × that close. Like CRSP/JKP `ME` it is an estimate. It equals actual
capitalisation when four conditions hold:
- the count is correct and covers the quoted class;
- shares changed between the count's date and S only by the splits the factor corrects;
- the factor is exact;
- the close is correct.

The acceptance gates below measure how far those conditions fail.

## The primitive: a raw price observed in SEC filings

A 13F-HR reports, per holding, shares (Column 5) and market value (Column 4). Form 13F Special Instruction 8 (SEC 1685,
07-23): "use the value at the close of trading on the last trading day of the calendar year or quarter … Enter values
rounded to the nearest dollar". Values are in dollars since 2023-01-03 (`sec-edgar.md` §7.1). PRN rows are dropped at
ingest (§7.2, `thirteen_f_normalise.py:81`).

Value ÷ shares is therefore a **reported raw quarter-end close**, an estimate of it subject to rounding and filer error,
on that date's share basis. Its ratio to the stored close on the same session measures eToro's re-basing of that
session since then:

F̂(q) = median over distinct 13F filers of (value ÷ shares) ÷ the stored close on the last trading session ≤ q.

**What the premise script observes.** These are observations, not a vendor contract (see "Checkpoint record"):
- **Splits and reverse splits.** The named series move F̂ by the split ratio, in the right direction, and back to about
  1 after the split. NFLX reads 10.0 until 2025-09-30 and 1.002 at 2025-12-31. BKNG reads 25 until 2026-03-31, KLAC 10
  until 2026-03-31, APH 2 until 2026-06-30, and DD 0.334 (a 1-for-3) until 2026-03-31. HON reads 0.50, but on
  2–26 filers, below the anchor minimum.
- **One spin-off.** FTV reads 1.001–1.016 across its Ralliant spin-off (2025-06-28). A price back-adjusted for the
  spin would read well above 1.
- **Spread under the anchor rule below**, measured exactly as specified: ≥ 20 filers on their latest-filed report, a
  close on s(q), and the coherence test.
  - Each quarter from 2024-06-30 has between 1,506 and 3,744 anchors, of which 1 to 35 are `incoherent`.
  - Of the `ok` anchors, 92.0–96.1% have m(F̂) ≤ 1.02.
  - Between 1 and 15 per quarter are ambiguous (1.10 < m < 1.25).
  - The largest m − 1 among snapped anchors is 0.100, at the band edge.

  These observations are unlabelled, so this is a spread, not a count of non-splits.

The NFLX share counts and the API and ToS facts above come from the queries and pages cited where they appear, not
from this script.

## Construction

### Anchors

Every calendar quarter end q from 2024-06-30 gets an **observation status** for instrument i. The anchor session s(q)
is the last NYSE session on or before q, from `market_calendar.us_market_status` (`nyse-market-calendar-v1`). The
status is `ok` when all of the following hold, and otherwise the named failure:
- `no_close`: i has no stored close on exactly s(q), or that close is NULL, not finite, or ≤ 0;
- `thin`: fewer than 20 distinct filers. A filer counts if it holds a row with finite shares > 0, finite value > 0 and
  no put/call, in its **latest accession for q** (an amendment replaces the filer's earlier report for that quarter),
  and it contributes the median of those rows;
- `incoherent`: fewer than 75% of filers lie within m ≤ 1.02 of the median, where m(x) = max(x, 1/x) of the
  filer-to-median ratio. A second cluster of a quarter of the filers is enough to fail;
- `anchor_ambiguous`: 1.10 < m(F̂) < 1.25.

For an `ok` status:
- m(F̂) ≤ 1.10 gives Φ = 1;
- m(F̂) ≥ 1.25 gives Φ = F̂, an estimate (KLAC 2026-03-31 reads 10.27). Its error carries into the cap unmeasured;
  see "Checkpoint record".

A quarter whose 13F due date (45 days after q; Rule 13f-1(a)(1)) has not passed is marked `provisional`. The daily
rebuild re-reads it as filings land. The status, F̂, filer count, coherence share and accession list are kept for
every quarter, `ok` or not.

### The factor for one count

The basis interval [b_lo, b_hi] is as in the source rule, compared on the anchor's observation session s(q), not the
quarter label. q_a is the last quarter with s(q_a) ≤ b_lo. The count's window W is every quarter from q_a to the first
quarter with s(q) ≥ b_hi, or to the latest quarter if there is none.

1. **Left edge.** The q_a status must be `ok`, else `no_left_anchor`.
2. **No bridging.** Every quarter in W must be `ok`. A `thin`, `incoherent`, `no_close` or `anchor_ambiguous` quarter
   inside W refuses the count (`basis_unresolved`). It is never skipped.
3. **Agreement.** max Φ / min Φ over W must be ≤ 1.05, else `basis_unresolved`. Then F = Φ(q_a).
4. **Right edge.** If W reaches a quarter with s(q) ≥ b_hi, the count is bracketed and rules 1–3 decide it.

   Otherwise W ends at the latest quarter. That quarter must be one of the two most recent quarter ends (no older
   than about 6 months), and Φ must be 1 across all of W, else `basis_unresolved`.

   The reasoning: Φ at the latest `ok` quarter is the net re-basing eToro has applied to that bar since. Under
   complete back-adjustment, it would be ≠ 1 after any net split that eToro has applied since then.

What these rules guarantee: no publication against an *observed* discrepancy (a quarter that disagrees, is missing, or
is ambiguous) inside the count's window. They do not prove the window was split-free. "Checkpoint record" lists what
they cannot see: offsetting events, a re-basing under 10%, and incomplete back-adjustment.

### Selection and steps

At session S:
1. Admitted counts: from accessions accepted before S (New York date < S) whose form is in scope.
2. Cover candidates: eligible by age, with the latest context, then the latest acceptance.
   - Distinct values for that (context, acceptance) give `shares_ambiguous`. There is no fallback past them.
3. Balance-sheet candidates: used only when no cover candidate is eligible. Same ordering and ambiguity rule.

A step is a maximal run of sessions with the same selected result. Its `valid_from` is the first session that result
is selected, which may be later than its acceptance when a cover count expires. Intervals are half-open and never
overlap, and a test asserts both. Expiry comes from the age predicate itself, tested at month ends and in a leap year.

**Step row** (`instrument_share_count_pit`):
- the selection: `instrument_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `shares_concept`, `accession`, `context_date`,
  `accepted_at`, `shares_reported`, all kept even when refused;
- the result: `split_factor`, or NULL with `refusal`. The refusals are:
  - `no_left_anchor` · `basis_unresolved`;
  - `shares_ambiguous` · `shares_scale_conflict` · `shares_turnover_implausible` · `shares_discontinuity`;
  - `multiclass_unavailable` · `invalid_input` (a non-finite or non-positive operand, or a non-finite product);
  - `anchor_contradicted` (Gate 1) · `float_bound` (Gate 2);
- `evidence` jsonb: the window's quarter statuses (q, s(q), status, filers, coherence share, F̂, provisional,
  accessions), each check's operands and outcome, and the discontinuity reference (its accession, context and count);
- `revision_count`: the number of `price_daily_revision` rows for the instrument visible to the build's snapshot. The
  table is append-only, so a newly committed row raises the count whatever its id. `max(revision_id)` is not
  commit-ordered and is not used;
- `build_id`.

The session-dependent check (turnover) is evaluated per session, so a step also ends where its outcome changes. The
discontinuity reference is the previous step's selected count on the same concept that published a factor. With no
such count, the check is `untested` and recorded so.

**`market_cap_pit_builds`** (append-only):
- `build_id`, start, finish, status;
- `code_sha` and `rule_version` (the sha256 of the frozen constants and the refusal set);
- `submissions_sha256` and the max `institutional_holdings.filed_at` in the snapshot.

### Build and publication

`market_cap_pit_rebuild` is a daily job after `daily_candle_refresh`. It runs in this order:
1. take the job lock, so builds serialise;
2. open one REPEATABLE READ transaction, so every database input is one snapshot;
3. build and run Gates 1 and 2 against the staged steps in that snapshot;
4. replace every step and mark the build complete, in the same transaction.

A failing gate does not abort the build. It sets that step's refusal, as above. A build that raises rolls back, and
the previous build stays active.

The scope is `strategies.validated_universe`'s US-equity source with a primary SEC CIK.

**`instrument_market_cap_pit` view.** It is `price_daily` ⋈ the step valid at `price_date`. It returns identity,
`close`, the step fields, and `market_cap`, where `market_cap = close × shares_reported × split_factor` or NULL with
`reason`. `reason` is the step's refusal, or `invalid_input` when `close IS NULL OR NOT isfinite(close) OR close <= 0`,
or `basis_stale`. `basis_stale` means either:
- the instrument's current revision count differs from the step's `revision_count`;
- an unresolved `price_series_break` exists.

A session with no step gets no view row; the coverage report accounts for it.

Price writes and their revision rows commit in one transaction (`market_data.py`, #2414). The build must enumerate
every `price_daily` write path and confirm each records a revision row. A path that does not record one is a blocker
for this ticket, not a limit.

The view is a current estimate, not an append-only record. The first consumer that decides on it must copy the whole
view row (cap or refusal, the step fields, `evidence` and `build_id`) into its own decision row in one statement. No
consumer ships with this ticket.

### Amendment 1 (#3902): conflicts untime one accession; predecessor filings are excluded; FPI and ADS listings refused

Build 6 (2026-10-19) refused 20 in-scope CIKs whole: 8 `submissions_accession_conflict`, 12 `untimed_accession`.
`scripts/measure_3902_premise.py` measures both over the full scope (5,329 instruments):
- **Conflicts.** In all 8, the conflicting accession appears twice with the same form and items and acceptance times
  exactly 4 or 5 hours apart, the New York UTC offset of the day (AMZN's year-2000 424B3s and SC 13G; Form 4s, 8-Ks,
  an S-3/A, a CORRESP, a 6-K elsewhere). Every pair falls on one New York date. None is an accession any of the
  instrument's share-count or public-float facts cites.
- **The same offset across CIKs.** The A/B below found the shape again between a successor's and a predecessor's
  listings of one accession: XOM's latest 10-Q, `0000034088-26-000093`, is listed by both, at `22:55:38Z` and
  `18:55:38Z`.
- **Untimed.** In all 12 the primary CIK is a new registrant (XOM `0002115436`, CLBK, XPRO, UROY and 8 more), and every
  untimed accession is listed by the instrument's one non-primary SEC CIK, the row the CIK upsert demotes to
  `is_primary = FALSE` when it replaces a CIK (#1173; `sec_identity.py`). XOM's are its `0000034088-…` 10-Ks and
  10-Qs, filed before the holding-company reorganisation. SEC's companyfacts for the successor CIKs do not carry
  these accessions: the counts are the predecessor registrant's own, which our ingest stored under the instrument.

Source: an accession number is "a unique identifier assigned automatically to an accepted submission by EDGAR" (SEC,
*Accessing EDGAR Data*). Its acceptance time and form are therefore properties of the submission, whichever CIK's
submissions list it. Nothing in the source says which of two differing records for one accession is right, and no
source rule fixes the share basis across a change of registrant. The rules below are chosen by construction.

Rules, replacing rule 5's "a conflicting accession refuses the CIK". They live in `pit_market_cap_build.py`, which
reads the raw submissions blocks itself and then hands the de-conflicted blocks to the frozen `parse_submissions`:
1. **A conflict untimes one accession.** The build's clock reads a record as (the New York date of its acceptance,
   its form): the source rule admits a filing on the first session after that date. Within one CIK's main file and
   overflow pages, an accession whose copies disagree on the clock is dropped, however many copies there are. It is
   never resolved to one of the records. Copies that agree on the clock are one record carrying the latest acceptance,
   whatever their `items` (the build reads none) or time of day; the 4- or 5-hour pairs above are such copies. The filter touches only rows of a block whose required columns are
   lists of equal length and whose accession and form are strings; every other row and block reaches the frozen parser
   unchanged, so each of rule 5's other integrity failures still refuses the instrument when it is the primary CIK's.
2. **A predecessor's filing is excluded, not timed.** The build also parses the submissions of each of the
   instrument's non-primary SEC CIKs. A share-count or public-float fact whose accession the primary CIK's submissions
   never list (before de-confliction) but a non-primary CIK's index does is the predecessor registrant's own filing,
   and is dropped before selection: it neither publishes nor refuses. The build never multiplies a predecessor-only
   count by the instrument's price, on any session, so no exchange ratio is needed. A non-primary CIK whose index
   fails integrity lists nothing, so its accessions stay unknown.
3. **Cross-CIK disagreement is a conflict.** An accession the primary index times and a non-primary index lists on a
   different clock is dropped as in rule 1. On the same clock, the later acceptance is kept.
4. **The CIK-whole refusal stands, and now covers floats on dropped conflicts.** A share-count fact whose accession is
   untimed in the merged sense (dropped under rules 1 or 3, an unparseable acceptance, or listed by no CIK of the
   instrument) refuses the instrument (`untimed_accession`): a newer count that cannot be timed may already have been
   public. A public-float fact on a dropped-conflict accession refuses it the same way, so Gate 2 never falls back to
   an older float because a conflict removed the newer one. Other untimed float accessions keep the existing
   behaviour (skipped).
5. **Multi-class across CIKs.** The curated multi-class test matches any of the instrument's SEC CIKs.
6. **FPI and ADS listings are refused** (`fpi_adr`), per instrument. The set is the existing detection: an `ads_ratio`
   row, `coverage.filings_status = 'fpi'`, or the ADR/ADS name marker (`xbrl_derived_stats`; sql/241's `fpi_adr`
   CTE). An ADS is quoted per depositary share while its cover count is ordinary shares, so `close × shares_reported`
   misstates the cap by the ratio (ONC 13, ZLAB 10, AKTX 2,000). The ratio cannot correct a dated cap: `ads_ratio`
   holds the current ratio only and forbids historical recompute (sql/240). Ratio-1 listings (TEVA, CRTO) are refused
   too, because today's ratio does not date a past one. Two ADR-marked names (MREO, NNDM) have no ratio row. Of the
   detected set, 7 instruments have steps in build 6 and none publishes.

The refusal set in the rule version gains `fpi_adr`. The build summary counts dropped conflicts and excluded
predecessor facts.

Accepted limits (Codex checkpoint 1, round 3):
- Rule 2 rests on co-registrant submissions listing the filing under every filer. A current-registrant count filed
  only under another CIK would be excluded, and an older own count selected; the age predicate and the anchors still
  apply to it.
- A predecessor accession that the primary also lists is kept, as before this amendment. So is a successor count
  accepted before the succession closed. Neither is new exposure.
- Excluding predecessor counts removes the first successor count's discontinuity reference (`untested`). The scale
  check, turnover, the anchors and Gates 1 and 2 still apply.
- FPI and ADS detection is current-state. A listing that was an ADS in the past and is not detected now is not refused.
- Float ties across accessions at one acceptance keep the existing ordering.
- The 12 instruments recovered under rule 2 have no cap before their current registrant's first eligible count.
- The build trusts the fact attribution of `financial_facts_raw`, as before.
- Keeping the later of two same-date acceptances can reorder two counts with one context accepted on one day; the
  session each is admitted on does not change.

### Amendment 2 (#3908): anchors read the canonical 13F observation layer

**Problem.** The anchor's 13F unit values come from `institutional_holdings`. data-engineer SKILL.md lists that table among the legacy typed tables, "still written by ingesters, NOT read by rollup post-#905". The canonical store of 13F-HR information-table positions is `ownership_institutions_observations`. Both are written through the same CUSIP map. The observation layer is also written by the bulk Form 13F Data Set drain (`sec_13f_dataset_ingest.ingest_13f_dataset_archive`), which reloads the map for every archive. So positions dropped while a CUSIP was unmapped are restored there for every quarter the drain admits. In the legacy table, only one accession per CUSIP is rewashed (#2328).

Measured by `PYTHONPATH=. uv run python scripts/measure_3908_premise.py` on the dev DB, 2026-10-20, on the steps covering that date. These are storage-coverage figures. Anchor-eligible counts come from the A/B.
- GOOGL's distinct 13F filers per quarter, legacy table against observation layer:
  - 2025-06-30: 21 against 4,993;
  - 2025-12-31: 58 against 5,731;
  - 2026-03-31: 69 against 5,628;
  - 2026-06-30: 2,391 in both.
- Of the 771 `no_left_anchor` steps, 687 have a `thin` left anchor. For 74 of them the observation layer holds ≥ 20 distinct filers at q_a with a positive EQUITY row.
- Of 8,110,804 legacy (instrument, quarter ≥ 2024-06-30, filer CIK) pairs with a positive EQUITY row, 0 are missing from the observation layer. This is pair membership. It is not reader output, because the latest report is selected before rows qualify.

**Source rule.** Form 13F Special Instruction 8 and the anchor definition are unchanged. What changes is the input population: the same rule now reads every stored filing rather than the legacy table's subset. The data-engineer two-layer rule fixes the observation layer as the read layer.

The normalisation is the writers' and is not repeated in the reader. Both 13F writers apply:
- PRN rows dropped at ingest (sec-edgar.md §7.2; `thirteen_f_normalise.py:81`). The bulk drain refuses a non-blank type other than `SH`; a blank type passes, and the per-filing parser defaults it to `SH`. This differs from §7.2's explicit-`SH` rule. It is not new exposure: the per-filing parser also writes the legacy table, so both readers carry it. It is recorded as a deviation in the limits below;
- values in dollars under the filing-date cutover (§7.1);
- sub-manager rows of one position summed into one row per (accession, instrument, exposure) (prevention log, 13F sub-manager aggregation).

**Rule.** The unit-value reader reads `ownership_institutions_observations`. Each column is the counterpart of the one it replaces:

| legacy column | observation-layer column |
|---|---|
| `filer_id` | `filer_cik` |
| `period_of_report` | `period_end` |
| `accession_number` | `source_accession` |
| `filed_at` | `filed_at` |
| `shares`, `market_value_usd` | `shares`, `market_value_usd` |
| `is_put_call IS NULL` | `exposure_kind = 'EQUITY'` |

The predicates apply in the legacy order:
1. **Row set before selection:** `source = '13f'`, `ownership_nature = 'economic'` and `known_to IS NULL` (the active economic rows; voting overlays and superseded rows are excluded), for the batch's instruments and quarters. All exposures and quantities stay in.
2. **Latest report:** per (filer, instrument, quarter), the rows at the maximum `filed_at` of that row set.
3. **Qualification after selection:** `exposure_kind = 'EQUITY'`, with the legacy predicates verbatim (shares > 0 and value > 0, neither NaN). The filer's value is the median value ÷ shares over the qualifying rows. A filer whose latest report has no qualifying row contributes nothing, as now.

The build stamp `holdings_max_filed_at` stays the maximum `filed_at` over the reader's qualifying output, as now; the column keeps its name. The rule version gains `"anchor_source": "ownership_institutions_observations"`.

**Accepted limits:**
- **Same-date reports.** `filed_at` is `TIMESTAMPTZ`, but the bulk drain writes the filing date at midnight. Reports filed on one date therefore tie, and all of their rows enter that filer's median, as in the legacy rule when two reports share a `filed_at`.
  - Measured over quarters ≥ 2024-06-30, on the reader's qualifying rows (`scripts/measure_3908_premise.py`): 30,142 (filer, instrument, quarter) groups have more than one contributing accession at the latest `filed_at`. In 5,270 of them the accessions' unit values differ by more than 2%, and in 3,939 by more than 10%.
  - The case inspected is a filer error, not an amendment: CIK 0000714364's two same-day reports for 2026-03-31 give META 0.74 and 0.57 per share, which is values in thousands.

### Amendment 3 (#3925): the scale check's comparator when the accession has no balance-sheet count

**Problem.** The scale check compares a cover count with the same accession's `us-gaap:CommonStockSharesOutstanding` (DQC_0095). When the build holds no usable comparator for that accession, the check is `untested`. An overstated count is then bounded only by the discontinuity check, and only when an effective reference exists: an earlier published step of the same concept with a different selection. Turnover catches only an understated cap, and Gate 2 is a lower bound. With no effective reference and no other refusal, the overstated count publishes, becomes the reference, and refuses the correct counts after it.

CPK (iid 9864) is the case. Its six 10-Q covers accepted (New York dates) from 2024-05-08 to 2025-11-06 carry 22,270,177,000 to 23,650,684,000 shares, while each period's weighted average and net income / EPS give 22.25M to 23.66M. CPK has no `CommonStockSharesOutstanding` row in `financial_facts_raw` at all. Under Amendment 2 the counts accepted 2025-08-07 and 2025-11-06 publish, and the correct 2026 counts are refused as `shares_discontinuity`.

**Measurement.** `PYTHONPATH=. uv run python scripts/measure_3925_premise.py --build-id 9 --trace 9864 8815 6104 1052454 4387 1531 10716`, output committed at `docs/research/3925-premise.txt` (one REPEATABLE READ snapshot, 2026-10-20 16:04Z). The script's `evaluate` implements rules 1–5 independently of the production module. Its unit is a distinct (instrument, cover accession) selected by a step of build 9 with a single count: 33,946 units, none with more than one selected count or stored scale outcome. Build 9 has 0 cover selections with no single count. An accession is "published" when any step selecting it publishes. These are static classifications of each accession. Publication effects come only from the A/B, which rebuilds the step sequence.
- **Rule-1-eligible:** 4,075 published accessions (4,070 with no balance-sheet count in the build's read, 5 with an invalid one) and 2,393 refused.
- **Not eligible:** 14,170 published accessions have a usable balance-sheet comparator and a stored `pass`. There, a candidate can be compared with the balance-sheet check. Neither side is independently verified, so the comparison is disagreement, not an error rate.

**Candidates measured and rejected** (100× against the cover count, on the not-eligible published accessions unless stated):
- **Weighted average alone, unchecked:** it disagrees with the balance-sheet `pass` on 103 of 13,988 accessions that have it. For example, iid 6104's accessions accepted 2024-05-10 to 2025-05-07 carry weighted averages of 145.2 to 151.6 against covers of 145M to 152M (trace).

- **A balance-sheet count from another accession:** CPK has none in any accession, so it cannot fix the case.

**Source rule.**
- **DQC_0095** ("Scale – Common Stock Outstanding"): a cover count and a share count in the same filing that differ more than 100× are a scale error. The tolerance and the same-filing scope are kept.
- **ASC 260-10-45-10**: basic EPS is income available to common stockholders divided by the weighted-average common shares outstanding. **DQC_0227** (`DQC.US.0227.10920`, effective 2026-06-01; `DQC_0227.xule`, 2026) tests that identity. It flags `EarningsPerShareBasic` when its decimals interval and the interval of `NetIncomeLossAvailableToCommonStockholdersBasic` / `WeightedAverageNumberOfSharesOutstandingBasic` are separated by more than a $0.01 per-share gap. It suppresses the flag for short FY (< 310 days), Q2 (< 120) and Q3 (< 200) periods on the filing's fiscal period focus, and for filings carrying a two-class-method EPS concept.
- **By construction** (no published rule): a project identity screen modelled on DQC_0227, and accepting a period that passes it as a same-filing comparator for DQC_0095, with the cover compared against both of its values. Passing the screen is not passing DQC_0227: the numerator and the two-class exclusion differ (below). The rule is frozen in `rule_version`.

**Rule.**
1. **Eligibility.** The selection is a cover count, its value is finite and positive, and `accession_sheet_count` over the build's existing fact read (which keeps contexts on or after 2023-04-01) gives no finite positive value. That means no balance-sheet count, more than one value at its latest instant, or one non-finite or non-positive value. Otherwise the scale check is unchanged.
2. **Facts.** The build reads, for every accession holding a cover fact in its read, the undimensioned `financial_facts_raw` rows (companyfacts drops dimensional facts, `sec-edgar.md` §7.17) of:
   - `WeightedAverageNumberOfSharesOutstandingBasic` (unit `shares`);
   - `EarningsPerShareBasic` (unit `USD/shares`);
   - `NetIncomeLossAvailableToCommonStockholdersBasic` and `NetIncomeLoss` (unit `USD`).

   Each row needs a start on or before its end and a non-NaN value. Rows are read by accession (`_IDENTITY_FACTS_SQL`), without the cover read's context filter, as `IdentityFact` rows carrying the concept, start, end, exact value and `fiscal_period` (companyfacts `fp`, the filing's fiscal period focus). Rows on a predecessor accession are skipped, as in `timed_share_facts` (`identity_facts`), and `build_steps` receives the rest keyed by accession. They are evaluated only for an eligible selection, against the selected (already timed) accession. Operand values are de-duplicated exactly, whatever their `fiscal_period`.

   The accession's **fiscal period focus** is the one distinct non-blank `fiscal_period` over its rows of the four concepts. With none, or more than one, the focus is unknown.
3. **Periods.** The latest end among the accession's weighted-average rows. With no weighted-average row, or a latest end after the accession's New York acceptance date, the accession has no period (`no_weighted_average`, `end_after_acceptance`). Each distinct start of a weighted-average row at that end is one period. Earlier ends are never used.
4. **A period is consistent** when all of these hold, and is otherwise unused with the first failing status:
   - `focus_unknown`: the accession's fiscal period focus is known;
   - `dqc_short_period`: its length (end − start + 1 days) is not under DQC_0227's minimum for that focus (FY 310, Q2 120, Q3 200; none for Q1 or another focus);
   - `operand_count`: it has exactly one weighted-average value, one EPS value and one numerator value. The numerator is `NetIncomeLossAvailableToCommonStockholdersBasic` when the period has that concept, else `NetIncomeLoss`. Two values of the preferred concept leave the period unusable, with no fallback;
   - `operand_invalid`: as floats, all three are finite, the weighted average is positive, EPS and the numerator are non-zero, and they share a sign. A loss period with both negative is usable;
   - `identity_disagrees`: |numerator − EPS × weighted average| ≤ 0.01 × weighted average in exact decimal arithmetic (so a gap of exactly one cent passes), and numerator / EPS is finite and within 100× of the weighted average (inclusive, in float).
5. **Outcome.** With no consistent period, this check does not refuse: it is `untested`. It **fails** when the cover count is more than 100× (exclusive) from the weighted average, or from numerator / EPS, of any consistent period, and **passes** otherwise. The comparisons use the existing `_within_tolerance`. A failure refuses `shares_scale_conflict` in the existing precedence, after `multiclass_unavailable`, `shares_ambiguous`, `invalid_input` and the basis refusals, with no recovery.

The 100× bound on numerator / EPS in rule 4 is what keeps the penny test meaningful for small EPS. At EPS 0.01, a weighted average 1,000× too large still moves numerator / weighted average by less than 0.01.

**Evidence.** The scale entry gains:

| case | `outcome` | `comparator` | `sheet_unavailable` | `periods` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| balance-sheet comparator usable | pass / fail | `balance_sheet` | null | absent |
| eligible, a consistent period | pass / fail | `weighted_average_basic` | `none` / `ambiguous` / `invalid` | every period |
| eligible, no consistent period | untested | null | as above | every period, possibly none |
| selection not a valid cover count | untested | null | null | absent |

**Departures from DQC_0227, and why:**
- **Intervals.** DQC_0227 builds each interval from the facts' `decimals`. `decimals` is NULL on every stored row of these concepts (145,846 EPS, 179,566 net income, 143,975 weighted-average rows). The screen uses the point values with the $0.01 tolerance. For the same operands it is stricter than DQC_0227: a pair DQC_0227 accepts only through rounding intervals is unused here, and an unused period does not make this check refuse.
- **Numerator.** The ingest stores only the concepts in `TRACKED_CONCEPTS` (`app/providers/implementations/sec_fundamentals.py:191,430`). `NetIncomeLossAvailableToCommonStockholdersBasic` is not among them and has 0 rows, so `NetIncomeLoss` is the numerator for every period today. It differs from income available to common by preferred dividends and participating allocations. The screen tests the substituted numerator against EPS. A difference can pass the penny test and still move numerator / EPS, which is a comparator, so it can change a cover's verdict while the period stays consistent. No paired measurement of the two numerators is possible on stored data, so this departure is unmeasured. It is bounded by rule 4: numerator / EPS must stay within 100× of the weighted average and within a cent per share of the identity.
- **Two-class filings.** The two-class EPS concepts are not tracked either and have 0 rows, so the exclusion cannot be read. A two-class filing whose `NetIncomeLoss` / weighted average matches EPS within a cent is used, where DQC_0227 would suppress its identity check. This departure is unmeasured. The weighted average itself is a count of the common shares under either method; the risk is in numerator / EPS, as for the numerator above.
