## Question

Does a long-only book of US large caps, **value-weighted** on market equity, selected by the industry-relative
composite of supported families that step 1 rebuilt faithfully, with step 2's band and exclusions plus a sector cap,
a name cap and #3621's MAX filter, over **24 months of forward paper accrual** that begin after its declaration is
frozen:
- (a) load on the factors it is built to carry (condition 3); and
- (b) beat SPY total return and a matched random book, net of our costs (condition 4)?

**What the answer can say.**
- **Pass:** the four conditions of `docs/settled-decisions.md` entry 2026-10-08 hold, with conditions 3 and 4 read
  on forward months that no person or process could read at the freeze. The verdict goes to the supervisor under
  that entry (#3740, 2026-10-10). This spec does not decide demo admission (§"After the verdict").
- **It does not prove a premium.** No realistic IR claim for this construction can be powered on 24 months
  (premise 2). The beats are point estimates.
  Under a normal, stationary active-return model with a true net IR of 0.3 against SPY,
  P(beat over 24 months) ≈ Φ(0.3 · √2) ≈ 0.66: a property of the screen, not this book's pass probability.
- **What a pass never authorises:** live capital (the entry's "Unchanged" list), or any construction other than the
  one frozen here.
- **Fail:** "not demonstrated" on #3740 and #3609. No variant is built because of this result.

**Estimand.** On the forward months: US exchange-listed common stocks that eToro lists, that link to a 10-K/10-Q
filer, cut to the 1,000 largest by ME each month, with entry restricted to names tradable at the decision session
(§"Population and eligibility"). This differs from step 2's estimand, whose population was the Intrader admission;
the difference is an explicit amendment, not an inheritance. ME keeps step 1's issuer-level share count applied to
the one linked security; for a firm with another listed or unlisted class that can overstate the security's ME, and
the exposure is not measurable with our data (step 1 §"Market equity", Scope). It affects forward rankings, value
weights and the cap reference. Nothing is generalised to CRSP.

- **The G1 bar, calibrated for 24 months.** At T = 24 with seven regressors, a normal bar on a Newey–West t is only
  nominal. For each evaluated set the planner simulates the null: 10,000 replications, each a 24-month circular
  block bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1992; block length 3, by construction) of the 119 historical months' joint rows
  of factors, RF and the arm's regression residuals: block starts are drawn uniformly from the 119 months, a block
  wraps from the last month to the first, and eight blocks are concatenated. The book's excess return is rebuilt with
  the intercept and every intended loading set to zero and the other coefficients at their historical estimates
  (the power simulation also sets the intercept to zero, so no alpha enters either); each replication runs
  G1's regression and records each intended loading's t. A replication that G1 would refuse (rank deficiency, zero
  residual variance, a non-finite or non-positive standard error) records t = +∞ for the null and counts as not
  clearing for power; the denominator is always 10,000. Quantiles are nearest-rank (the ⌈q · 10,000⌉-th order
  statistic). Per loading, the bar is the larger of z(1 − 0.05/k) and the simulated (1 − 0.05/k) quantile of that
  loading's null t, the larger across the two arms. Each (set, arm, null or power) run has its own
  `random.Random(f"3740-step3-planning:{set}:{arm}:{kind}")`, drawing each replication's eight starts in order, with
  `set` the families joined by `+` in the table's order. The bars are written to the plan file and frozen by the
  declaration; G1 uses them.
- **Power, by the same bootstrap** with the intended loadings at their historical estimates: the fraction of
  replications in which every intended loading clears its bar in the arm, for each arm. **Criterion:** the
  conjunction bound over the two arms, 1 − Σ (1 − arm power), at least 0.80. The analytic planned t
  b / (s · √(119 / 24)) and its normal power, and the bootstrap power at each loading's unadjusted lower bound
  (b − 1.645 s), are printed beside it, not gated.
- **Assumptions, stated:** the forward months resemble the historical path's joint factor and residual behaviour up
  to the block length; the population differs (premise 4), which the bootstrap does not model; the first set to
  pass is chosen, so its historical loadings carry some selection optimism. The powers are conditional planning
  figures, not guarantees.
- **Refusals:** step 2's planner's (a missing factor month, a path off its 119 holding months, an `INSUFFICIENT`
  formation, a non-finite or non-positive standard error).
- **If no set passes,** step 3 stops before any declaration ("condition 3 cannot be powered on 24 months"); nothing is

- **Why reading 119 historical months here is allowed.** The selection reads exposure statistics of months that the
  test never uses. Exposures carry information about performance, so the historical path is not information-free;

## Condition 1: post-publication support, decided before planning

Condition 1 needs independent post-publication support for **every family in the book**, and the issue asks for it
per member (#3740 body, decision rule 1). The rule below is fixed by construction (no source gives a numeric
acceptance rule) on step 2's precedent: the premium "stayed positive after its US publication and in markets outside
the US" (step 2 §"Adoption rationale").

- **Series:** for each member characteristic, the long-short factor return Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen publish for that
  exact characteristic and sign, in the capped-value-weight form step 1's fidelity bars read: (i) the US series and
  (ii) JKP's regional series for developed markets excluding the US. Each original paper's publication year and
  sample are JKP's factor-details record. Slice 2 fetches one vintage of each file, pins every file by sha256, and
  records the exact series keys and the regional definition JKP states.
- **Windows:** (i) every calendar month from the January after the original paper's publication year through
  2024-08; (ii) every calendar month from the series' first published month through 2024-08. Confirmation is forward,
  so no separation reason stops the window at our path's start; the end is fixed at 2024-08 whatever the vintage.
- **Independence:** (i) is out of sample in time; (ii) is out of sample in geography only if the original paper used
  no data from those markets. Slice 2 records each original paper's sample markets from the paper; if it included
  any developed ex-US market, (ii) does not count for that member and (i) alone decides.
- **A member is supported** when each counting window has at least 24 months and a positive mean monthly return. A
  family is supported when every member is. Newey–West t, month counts and means are printed; significance is not
  required, because neither our test nor this rule measures the premium (premise 2). No post-sample substitute is
  allowed: a member with fewer than 24 post-publication US months is unsupported.
- **Data validity:** a counting series must have exactly one finite value for every calendar month of its window,
  through 2024-08; a series that ends earlier, or is empty, non-finite, duplicated or gapped, or cannot be fetched,
  makes the member unsupported.
- **Order of operations:** this table is computed and committed before the planner runs; premise 5's candidate list
  is filtered to supported families before evaluation, and families removed this way are not searches.
- **The prior, stated:** small positive long-short premia with wide uncertainty and post-publication decay (McLean &
  Pontiff 2016, 58% on average, cited not measured). The long-only, value-weighted large-cap book's net share is
  smaller and unmeasured.

**Fixed by construction** (each frozen in the construction-version hash):
- **Composite with several families.** A name has a composite when a **strict majority** of the chosen families
  have a family score (1 of 1, 2 of 2, 2 of 3); its input is the mean of the family scores present, standardised by
  step 2's one-population rule. This is step 2's first-draft rule for three families (`MIN_FAMILIES = 2`, removed by
  #3718 when the book became value-only) extended to every set size. Membership patterns are counted and printed per
  formation, as step 2 prints them.
- **The cap solution.** Targets T at M (n = |T| ≥ 1), u_i = ME_i / Σ_{j∈T} ME_j, g(i) the FF-12 group (the
  unclassified bucket is a group), c_g = 2 W_g with W_g the group's ME share of the cap reference, name cap 0.10.
  - For a group g and scale s ≥ 0, f_g(s) = Σ_{i∈g} min(0.10, s u_i): continuous, non-decreasing, with breakpoints at
    s = 0.10 / u_i, saturating at 0.10 n_g.
  - A group's weight at a global scale θ is min(f_g(θ), c_g). If f_g(θ) ≤ c_g, its names take w_i = min(0.10, θ u_i).
    Otherwise its names take w_i = min(0.10, s_g u_i), with s_g the smallest scale at which f_g(s_g) = c_g; it exists
    because f_g is continuous from 0 and f_g(θ) > c_g.
  - θ is the smallest scale with Σ_g min(f_g(θ), c_g) = 1. It is solved exactly over the merged breakpoints of every
    f_g and every group's cap-crossing point (each piece is linear in θ).
  - **This allocation is authoritative.** It is this spec's reading of the issue's "excess redistributed pro rata,
    iterated" for both caps at once. A sequential clip-and-redistribute loop can end elsewhere depending on pass
    order (checkpoint 1 round 1, finding 9, gives an example), and is not used.
  - **Tests:** Σ w + cash = 1 to 1e-12; each cap holds to 1e-12; within each group, w_i / u_i is equal across names
    not at the name cap; across groups not at their sector cap, the common scale is θ.
- **Infeasible caps leave cash.** If Σ_g min(c_g, 0.10 n_g) < 1, each group takes min(c_g, 0.10 n_g): when 0.10 n_g
  binds every name takes 0.10; when c_g binds the names follow s_g above. The residual 1 − Σ w is cash at a zero
  return until the next formation. Formation, residual and binding groups are printed. A long-only book under a hard
  cap holds the residual; it is not a refusal.
- **Cash in the path.** `factor_book_path.value_path` today refuses weights that do not sum to 1 (`_valid_weights`,
  `:337-343`) and holds no cash while targets exist. Slice 2 extends `Decision` with an explicit cash weight: target
  values are w_i × post-cost NAV and cash is the residual × post-cost NAV; carried cash, terminal and coverage-exit
  proceeds and sale proceeds join cash before the next formation's one-pass rebalance; cash trades cost nothing and
  are excluded from turnover. Tests assert NAV conservation (positions + cash before trades = after trades + costs)
  at every formation, for the book and the control.
- **Empty targets:** zero targets leave the book all cash (step 2 §"Insufficient book"); no weights are computed.
- **The cap reference** is the formation's top 1,000, cap-weighted on ME at s(M): step 2's reconstituted
  cap-weighted universe at that formation.
- **Caps hold at formations only.** Weights drift between rebalances, so a name can exceed 10% and a group its cap
  within a month; the 5% gap budget holds immediately after each rebalance. Printed per formation: the largest name
  and group weights before that formation's trades (the drifted weights the monthly returns imply), against the
  previous formation's caps.

sale at s(M) at the exchange closing print the capture holds, under step 0's market-on-close assumption, which
models the exchange print, not the broker's availability. There is therefore no locked position: a holding either
has a valid close at s(M) and is sold or kept at it, or its bars stopped and step 1's classifier has already realised
it to cash in the holding month (`terminal` or `coverage_exit`, an interior gap included). A holding with no valid
close at s(M) that step 1 did not realise is step 2's `PRICE_INVALID`, which at a forward formation makes that
formation missed (§"Lifecycle and verdict table").

## Decision rule (frozen before any outcome)

The book passes only if all four conditions of the 2026-10-08 entry hold.
1. **Published support:** §"Condition 1", for every family in the chosen set, committed before planning.
2. **Construction fidelity:** step 1's run `b6378c7c` passed every member of the chosen set.
3. **Powered exposure test (G1), on the 24 forward holding months.** Regress the book's monthly return in excess of RF
   on FF5 plus momentum, with step 0's unit checks and Newey–West rule (lag ⌊4(24/100)^(2/9)⌋ = 2). Each intended
   loading must be positive with a one-sided t above its frozen bar from the plan file (premise 5: at least
   z(1 − 0.05/k), raised to the bootstrap null quantile where that is larger), in **both** termination arms, at base
   cost. Its power is premise 5's. Step 2's G1 refusals apply (`G1_REFUSED`).
4. **Net beat on the forward sample (G2).** Over the same 24 months, in **both** arms at base cost:
   - the book's annualised net log growth G exceeds B1's by more than the margin over B1; and
   - exceeds the median (nearest-rank) of the control's 1,000 net G values by more than the margin over the control.
   - **Margins: zero over both comparators,** the entry's floor, on step 2's basis: no source gives an expected net
     excess for this construction, and over 24 months a realistic edge is far inside the sampling noise.

Step 2's rules on complete series, G as the comparison metric, `WEALTH_NONPOSITIVE` and `COMPARATOR_INVALID` apply,
with "the 119 months" read as the 24 forward months.

**Selection across configurations.** One configuration is evaluated on the forward sample, so PBO/CSCV, which needs
at least two, is not computed, and a beat shown by only one of several configurations cannot arise. Premise 5's
evaluated sets read only historical months and are counted in the register.

**The turnover veto** applies to the forward formations: any forward formation (the initial purchase excluded, as in
step 0) with one-way turnover above 50% at base cost in either arm fails the run (`TURNOVER_BENEFIT_UNSHOWN`).

**Printed beside the verdict, never gating:** stress cost; each forward year; segments; cap, cash and filter counts;
the 119-month historical regression and the historical path's attribution and diagnostics, labelled "development
data used in design; step-3 outputs computed after the declaration; not confirmation". Step 2's "fails at stress
cost" annotation applies to the forward G2. **"depends on <year>"** keeps step 2's calendar-year partition: each
calendar year the 24 forward months touch (partial endpoint years included) is deleted in turn from the book, B1 and
every control draw, and the remaining months are annualised.

## Forward accrual

**Grid and endpoints.** M_0 is the first month-end whose s(M) falls after the declaration merges on `main`;
formations M_0 .. M_23, holding months M_0 + 1 .. M_0 + 24. The grid is fixed: it never moves. s(M) is the last
session of M on a frozen exchange session calendar (slice F pins it); if the capture has no SPY bar for that session,
the formation is missed (`SESSION_MISSING`), never moved to an earlier bar.
- **Start.** The book, every control draw and both references start all cash at s(M_0). The **initial purchase** is
  the first formation at which a series buys anything: if M_0 is missed, they stay all cash through its holding
  month and make the initial purchase at the next captured formation (the miss counts toward `ACCRUAL_GAPS`). Slice
  2 categorises it `INITIAL_PURCHASE`, excluded from turnover, replacing `value_path`'s `index == 0` test. Cost
  attribution keeps the path's timing: a purchase at M_0 is charged in holding month M_0 + 1's return (as step 2
  charges its 2014-09-30 purchase in October 2014); a delayed first purchase at M_j, j > 0, is charged in month M_j's
  return, like every later formation's trades.
- **End.** At s(M_0 + 24), the last session of the 24th holding month, every position is sold at its band with no
  rebalance first, charged and excluded from turnover (step 0).
- **B1** is bought at s(M_0) and sold at s(M_0 + 24), each charged at the band of SPY's raw close at s(M_0) (step 2's
  one-band rule). That close is acquired and bound in M_0's formation window under its own key (`b1_entry_close`),
  whether or not M_0's formation is captured. If it cannot be bound in that window the trial is `REFUSED`
  (`B1_UNDEFINED`) before any holding month is read; nothing has been evaluated, so a new declaration with a later
  M_0 costs no sample.
- The report refuses a forward path whose formations differ from M_0 .. M_23 (`ReportError`, as step 2's
  `STAGE_GRIDS`).

**Missed formation transition.** For the book, every control draw and both references: no trades; positions keep
their values and entry bands, cash is carried, terminal and coverage-exit proceeds join cash, and every carried
holding still takes a holding-month return from the returns capture, whatever its universe status. Slice 2 adds this
transition to `value_path` with tests; it is not an omitted formation.

**Sealing invariants.** Slice F's spec designs the capture protocol (binding keys, windows, receipts, witness,
retries) and its checkpoint 1 checks it against these invariants; this spec fixes only the invariants.
1. **Cutoff.** Every input a formation uses is bound (an immutable digest with an independently witnessed time)
   before its cutoff, in exactly one of two categories, and slice F assigns every component to one:
   - **mutable decision metadata** (the eToro listing, membership, tradability and instrument type, CIK links, the
     companyfacts bulk files) and the price and MAX inputs of sessions before s(M): bound by acquisitions made
     before the close of s(M), each bound when made;
   - **the decision session's price package**: the closing prints of s(M) and whatever adjustment inputs slice F's
     accepted sources need for s(M)'s daily total return (the last MAX observation) and its screens, bound within a
     post-close window ending before the next session's open.

   The formation manifest references those existing bindings.
   Sources versioned with knowledge timestamps (EDGAR filings and headers by acceptance time) are read as of step 1's
   evidence cutoff and need no snapshot.
2. **Immutability.** A bound acquisition is never replaced. Validation reads bound bytes; a retry acquires only
   components still missing, under a fixed source precedence, and cannot alter a bound component. Slice F lists
   which defects refuse an input permanently and which can be completed by later evidence.
3. **One binding per identity.** Each binding's identity names the trial, the capture kind, the source or dataset,
   and the period (an observation date, a month, or a publication vintage, as the kind defines). A second binding for
   one identity triggers step 2's rule: every later report refuses (`CAPTURE_AMBIGUOUS`) until re-declaration.
4. **Every window is executable:** start, end, timezone and retry rule for each capture kind, with acquisition and
   witness-publication deadlines separated, and a missed acquisition deadline final.
5. **The witness is append-only or its history is retained.** Either an append-only external witness, or a witness
   whose creation time, original digest and every later edit or deletion are captured and checked by the verdict
   run; publication failure, duplicate publication, edit, revert and deletion are exercised in the dry run.
6. **Cross-source splicing** meets `research-process.md`'s splicing rule (identity mapping, adjustment basis,
   overlap reconciliation, vendor precedence) across formation closes, MAX bars, share-count split adjustments and
   holding returns; any substitution is a declared exception.
7. **No interim result.** No book, control or reference return is computed for any forward month until the 24th
   month's returns are bound. Each capture checks only input integrity (counts, refusals, coverage).
8. **Access rows.** Each acquisition attempt writes a `record_holdout_access` row for `3740-step3-book`
   (`access_kind="read"`, `result_version` = the attempt id) before it reads; the verdict run writes the `evaluate`
   row. Step 2's abandoned-run classifier applies per attempt id.

**Formation finality.** A formation's inputs are bound by its cutoff (invariant 1), but whether it is valid depends
on the previous holding month's statuses (`still_held`, `factor_book_path.py:209`): a holding without a valid close at
s(M) is already cash if step 1's classifier realised it, and otherwise refuses `PRICE_INVALID`. So the formation
binds inputs for the whole required set (the population, every name any series holds, the control draws' holdings
and both references' constituents), and its validity is resolved deterministically once the previous month's
returns are bound (below), from bound inputs only. `formation_captured` or `formation_missed` is final at that point.

**Returns window.** Holding month M + 1's returns use evidence available at its cutoff, the tenth session after the
month's last session (time for Form 25 and termination filings and dividend records to appear). For each holding,
step 1's classifier runs first on that evidence: `terminal` follows step 1 and step 0 (both arms); `coverage_exit`
applies only under step 1's coverage condition (the series' bars stop without a terminating event, or an interior
gap), valued at its last valid bar; otherwise the holding is `observed` and needs a valid total return. A missing or
invalid total return for an `observed` holding is an incomplete input, never a coverage exit: the month is
completed under invariant 2, within `INPUT_UNAVAILABLE`'s nine-month limit.

**B1 and factors.** For each factor dataset (five-factor, momentum) the evaluation input is the **first accepted
`french_reference_refresh` snapshot covering all 24 forward months**; earlier snapshots are provenance only. For B1,
each forward month's IVV Item B.5.a return binds at the first N-PORT load that brings it; later revisions are
provenance only. Each binding is made at ingestion (invariants 1–5), and the verdict verifies existing bindings.

**Pinned runtime.**
- **Code:** captures and the verdict run execute the declared commit, from a dedicated worktree at the declaration's
  tag with the locked dependencies, invoked by one scheduled job. The construction hash is computed over that tree,
  so routine changes elsewhere cannot invalidate the trial.
- **Ledger:** binding is read from slice F's binding records and from a separate read-only checkout of `origin/main`
  fetched by each attempt; the attempt records both the code tag and the ledger commit it read.
- **Database interface:** slice F lists every table and column a capture reads; each attempt hashes that slice of
  `information_schema` and refuses (`SCHEMA_DRIFT`, an integrity failure, retryable) if it differs from the hash the
  declaration pins. External services (eToro, SEC, French, N-PORT) are inputs, captured and hashed, not code.
- **Repairs:** a retry with identical code is operational. Any change to the pinned tree is a new declaration with
  its own, later M_0; no bound period of this trial is reused by it, because its months would then have been read
  before that declaration's freeze.
- Slice F names the job lane and its connection budget (the dev cluster runs at `max_connections` = 30 with no
  headroom).

**Slice F's obligations.** Slice F is the forward capture's own spec, Codex checkpoint 1, code and dry run. For each
input it either reproduces the cited rule exactly from a point-in-time source, or declares an exception that its
checkpoint reviews. The declaration cannot freeze until slice F's spec is closed and its dry run is accepted. Its
inputs:
1. **Security type:** a classifier with accepted and rejected classes (common stock against ETF, fund, commodity
   pool, preferred, warrant, unit, right, ADR treatment stated), its fields (eToro instrument type checked first, then
   SEC filer and SIC evidence), an unknown-type rule, primary-listing and RTH treatment, validated over every
   candidate instrument and applied to future entrants by the same rule (step 2 §"After a pass").
2. **Listing age:** an effective-dated, security-level listing history (candidate: the Form 8-A 12(b) registration
   in EDGAR submissions) with a stated missing-history policy; step 2 §"Source rules" shows archive seasoning is not
   listing age.
3. **SIC:** the SIC of the latest 10-K- or 10-Q-family accession accepted before s(M), read from that accession's
   EDGAR header (`STANDARD INDUSTRIAL CLASSIFICATION`, the Commission's assignment as of the filing date, available
   at acceptance), with step 1's `sic_null` for a header without one and `sic_unloaded` for an accession whose header
   cannot be read. This is a **declared source exception**: step 1's field is SUB `sic`, and slice F must cite EDGAR's
   documentation of the header field's meaning and availability, freeze the adoption rule before comparing, and
   report disagreements and unmatched accessions over every accession in the quarters both cover. Agreement there is
   compatibility evidence, not proof of equivalence elsewhere.
4. **Accounting:** a companyfacts bundle per formation with step 1's evidence cutoff and four-month lag.
5. **Shares and ME:** step 1 §"Market equity" exactly (cover-count precedence, the 15-month age limit, blocked-read
   handling, context-date against acceptance-date split bases); no lag on shares.
6. **Raw close at s(M):** an unadjusted decision-session print from an authoritative source or a validated, dated
   adjustment ledger, with first-capture and new-instrument handling and whole-population reconciliation. One
   candidate is a daily append-only capture of each session's close taken in its daily window, dating split
   events by the change in the captured-to-adjusted ratio between sessions; it qualifies only if slice F shows from
   eToro's documentation and a capture-time contract that a bar fetched on its own session is on the unadjusted
   basis. Quiet dry-run dates do not establish that.
7. **Split events:** dated split stamps covering every candidate back to its share-count basis date (up to 15
   months), with step 1's convention fixtures and population reconciliation.
8. **MAX inputs:** the daily return schema and #3621's screens (adjacent-session returns, zero counts, return and
   ratio screens) for the whole MAX population, including pre-month boundary bars.
9. **Holding-month returns:** total returns with dividends at the ex-date, on step 0's gross, pre-withholding
   research convention (step 2 §"Diagnostics", Operations); live-like accounting (`research-process.md` §"Returns")
   is a demo-test matter. Candidates: eToro price return plus captured cash dividends; a captured PWB revision's
   `adj_close`. Slice F freezes the independent reference, the population, the tolerances and the refusal rules
   before comparing, and tests splits, dividends, terminations and daily returns separately. Dry-run agreement is
   compatibility evidence, not a population guarantee.
10. **Terminations:** step 1's status classifier on Form 25 and filing evidence for a security that stops trading;
   a price-coverage failure is `coverage_exit`; broker eligibility loss is neither (§"Population and eligibility").
11. **B1:** IVV's N-PORT Item B.5.a returns, loaded on a schedule as each quarter's N-PORT dataset publishes
    (§"B1 and factors"), and SPY's raw close for the bands.
12. **Factors:** §"B1 and factors", verified as step 2 verifies snapshots 39 and 40.

**Dry run (prospective).** Slice F's captures run on at least three consecutive month-ends before the declaration,
as real point-in-time captures (retrospective reads cannot recreate a past source state); a retrospective plumbing
test on stored data may precede them and validates code only. The integrity reports cover population and top-1,000
counts, refusals by code, input coverage and the source selections. No book is formed on dry-run months. The
earliest declaration is therefore after the third dry-run month-end.

**Parity table** (printed, never gating). For the latest dry-run formation: the forward top 1,000 by exchange, by
step-1 exclusion reason and by FF-12 group, beside the same counts at the last stage-B formation (2024-07-31), and
the share of the population with each input present. It shows the size of the population change premise 5's
scaling does not model.

## After the verdict

- **Pass:** the supervisor reviews it against the 2026-10-08 entry. Whether a forward paper sample meets that entry's
  "historical screen" wording for a demo admission is decided there; premise 3 is the argument for it (the sample is
  sealed by time). A demo test, if admitted, is a separate claiming declaration with its own `TrialDesign`, a
  prediction interval from this accrual's monthly series and the #2500 sequential stop rule.
- **Fail, `INSUFFICIENT`, `G1_REFUSED` or `REFUSED`:** "not demonstrated". `G1_REFUSED` prints the path and G2's
  inputs with no gate verdict (step 2's rule); a correctable input problem behind it (an unbound factor snapshot) is
  an `INPUT_UNAVAILABLE` case before the verdict, not a `G1_REFUSED`. A new configuration is a new counted declaration and
  needs a reason other than this result.

## Known limits

- No IR claim (premise 2). G2 is a 24-month point-estimate screen with the ≈ 0.66 caveat at a true IR of 0.3.
- G1's power is planned from historical exposures and a population that differs from the forward one; the planned
  powers are approximate and the HAC bar at T = 24 is asymptotic.
- The historical path's data limits (survivorship verified only from 2019 against a selected Form 25 set;
  retrospective masks; no eToro eligibility; no security-type classifier) bound the planning and the diagnostics,
  not the test. The construction limits continue forward: the restricted 10-K/10-Q filer estimand, the top-1,000
  size proxy instead of NYSE breakpoints, and issuer-level share counts.
- The forward population (eToro listing) is not the historical one (Intrader admission).
- Issuer-level share counts on one linked security can overstate ME for multi-class firms, forward as historically;
  not measurable with our data.
- The G1 bar and power come from a block bootstrap of historical rows; they are calibrated to that history, not
  guaranteed for the forward months.
- The design responds to stages A and B; nothing on those stages confirms it.
- Value weighting does not make selection the only active bet: universe, sector, beta and style differences remain,
  and the attribution reports each against B1.
- Costs: step 0's nine summer calibration dates and stock bands; market-on-close fills are an assumption.
- The accrual's start waits for slice F's prospective dry run.
